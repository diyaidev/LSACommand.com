Why reviews matter so much in LSA

Google ranks Local Services Ads in an auction. Your rating and number of reviews count toward profile quality, one of the factors Google weighs alongside your bid and how likely your ad is to get a lead (Google: About ad rankings). Google recommends at least 5 reviews, and some business types need 5 before their ad can show (Google: Improve your Local Services Ads performance). Reviews are also what homeowners compare first when three providers show up side by side.

Most contractors know this and still don’t ask consistently. The crew forgets, the office is busy, and a generic review-request blast gets ignored.

A real conversation, not a review blast

  1. AI follows up after the job by phone call or text and asks how it went, like a good office manager would.
  2. It asks every customer, not just the happy ones. Google’s review policy doesn’t allow asking only satisfied customers, so every finished job gets the same request.
  3. It sends the link while they’re still engaged. Your Google review link arrives by text during the conversation, and the customer writes the review in their own words.
  4. Unhappy customers also reach the owner. If someone had a bad experience, you get the conversation so you can fix it personally. Google doesn’t allow discouraging negative reviews, so they get the same review link as everyone else.

Every review answered for you

When reviews come in, LSA Command replies to each one under your business name with a quick, professional response. That shows future customers you’re paying attention, and replying to reviews is one of Google’s own best practices.

Frequently asked questions

Do reviews affect Google LSA ranking?

Yes. Google counts your rating and number of reviews toward profile quality, one of the factors in the Local Services Ads auction alongside your bid and how likely your ad is to get a lead.

What does Google allow when asking for reviews?

Google encourages sharing your review link with customers and replying to reviews. Its policy prohibits offering incentives for reviews, discouraging negative reviews, asking only happy customers, and requesting that specific content be included in a review.

What happens if a customer is unhappy?

The owner gets the conversation so the problem can be fixed personally. The customer still gets the same review link as everyone else.
