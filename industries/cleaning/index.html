Which cleaning categories Google lists

Cleaning isn’t one category. Google’s US list has separate categories for each of these: (Local Services Help)

When we checked the US list in November 2026, we didn’t find pressure washing as its own category, so if that’s a client’s main service, check eligibility before promising anything. Pick the category for the service that makes the most money, not every service on the menu. A company that does house and carpet cleaning is working with two categories, each with its own settings.

Verification for cleaners

This is lighter than for plumbers or HVAC. For house cleaning, Google’s US requirements page lists a business background check, an owner background check, business registration, general liability insurance and state-level business and owner licenses, each marked “Select users only.” Carpet and upholstery cleaning, window cleaning, pool cleaners and junk removal list the same set. For HVAC, Google adds “service professional background checks,” because it treats HVAC as an urgent category. It doesn’t list that per-worker check for house cleaning. (Local Services Help)

Your own account is still the final word. Google says to “Review your Local Services dashboard to confirm the requirements for your business.” Once an account has moved into Google Ads, the old dashboard redirects there, so look at the Advertiser Verification tab instead. (Local Services Help) And if the account was verified before it moved into Google Ads, Google says the verified status and badge carry over, and insurance and license reverification are no longer required. (Google Ads Help) Our LSA verification checklist walks through the documents, and the LSA categories guide covers eligibility for every trade.

Repeat clients and the 15-day rule

Cleaning lives on recurring business, so this rule matters more here than in most trades. Google says: “Once a lead is charged, any follow-up calls or messages with that customer are not charged again, provided there is interaction within 15 days and the contact comes from the same phone number or email address. If 15 days pass with no activity, a new call or message from that customer creates a new lead and will be charged if it meets valid lead criteria.” (Google Ads Help)

Picture a monthly client who never saved your number, searches your company name, and taps call on your ad. After 15 quiet days, that can be a new paid lead. Two fixes:

  • Give every recurring client your direct number. Put it on the invoice, the reminder text and anything else they keep.
  • Make a deliberate call on Direct Business Search. Google says it “displays your ad exclusively for brand-name searches and screens out returning customers so you are only charged for new leads.” (Google Ads Help) For a company with a big recurring book, it’s worth deciding on purpose rather than leaving it at whatever was set.

When you can’t pick up

Every cleaning owner knows this one: gloves on, phone buzzing in a pocket. Google says “If a call goes to voicemail or an automated greeting during your business hours, it’s treated as a connected call and is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message.” Business hours “are the hours you set in your Google Business Profile,” and if your ad schedule is different, “unanswered calls are only charged when they happen during your active ad schedule and within your business hours.” (Google Ads Help) Three tools help:

ToolWhat Google saysWhen it fits
Ad scheduleUnanswered calls are only charged inside both the ad schedule and business hoursSet it to the hours someone can actually answer
Message leadsTwo types, a standard message or Request a Quote; typically priced lower than phone leads, though not alwaysOnly if someone can reply within 24 hours
Booking leadsUS and Canada, for accounts with an active booking integration through a supported scheduling partnerIf you already book online through a supported partner

On messages, Google says “Message leads are typically priced lower than phone leads, although that is not always the case,” and names the lead types as “standard message lead or Request a Quote.” (Google Ads Help) It also says to “Opt into message and booking goals if you have the capacity to respond to customer inquiries within 24 hours.” (Google Ads Help) Quotes suit cleaning well. For booking, appointments and availability are managed in the booking partner’s system. (Local Services Help) None of this replaces answering the phone: Google says “Phone calls are mandatory.” (Google Ads Help) More in our guides to LSA missed-call charges and responding to LSA message leads.

Service types and service area

Turn on only the jobs the company wants [CONFIRM: read the current house cleaning service-type names off a live or demo account before publishing. Google’s help pages don’t publish the list, so this page describes the work instead of naming the settings.]. If a client doesn’t do move-out cleans or one-time deep cleans, turn them off: Google no longer supports credits for “job type not serviced” and “geo not serviced” leads. (Local Services Help)

For the service area, Google says “do not set service area boundaries that are more than two hours of driving time from your business location.” (Google Ads Help) For cleaners, go much tighter than that. Drive time between houses is where cleaning margins go, so pick the towns your crews are already in. A town you won’t drive to is just a cost.

And before switching everything off for the holidays: “Deselecting all job types won’t stop you from getting leads on the overall category.” (Local Services Help) To stop all leads, pause the campaign. Our guide on how to pause Google Local Services Ads covers the slow season.

Reviews, photos and callouts

The profile is where a cleaning company wins the click.

  • Reviews. Google’s best practices point to consistent positive reviews “with ratings above four stars.” (Google Ads Help) The trap we see in cleaning is “$10 off your next clean for a review.” Google says offering incentives, such as free or discounted services, in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. (Google Business Profile Help) Send Google’s review link or QR code after every job and just ask.
  • Photos. Google suggests photos “such as employees performing work, or before and after photos,” and before-and-after is made for cleaning. It also says “personally identifying photos shouldn’t be uploaded,” so no client faces, house numbers or family photos in the shot. (Local Services Help) You can upload “up to 100 additional custom photos” to the campaign. (Google Ads Help)
  • Callouts. Google lets you “Select up to 6 callouts per business category,” such as free estimates or locally owned. (Google Ads Help) Only claim what’s true, and keep them current.

Frequently asked questions

Is house cleaning eligible for Google Local Services Ads?

Yes, in the US. Google’s US category list includes house cleaning services, carpet cleaning services and window cleaning services as separate categories, along with pool cleaner, junk removal services and water damage services. We didn’t find pressure washing as its own category on the US list, so check eligibility before promising it to a client.

Do cleaning companies need background checks for every employee?

Google’s US requirements page lists business and owner background checks, business registration, general liability insurance and state-level licenses for house cleaning, each marked for select users only. Unlike HVAC, it doesn’t list background checks for every service professional. Google applies checks case by case, so confirm the requirements in your own account: the Advertiser Verification tab in Google Ads, or the Local Services dashboard if the account hasn’t migrated yet.

Will I be charged again when a past cleaning client calls through my ad?

Possibly. Google says follow-up calls or messages from a charged customer aren’t charged again if there is interaction within 15 days from the same phone number or email. If 15 days pass with no activity, a new call or message from that customer creates a new lead and is charged if it meets valid lead criteria. Give recurring clients your direct number, and decide deliberately on Direct Business Search, which Google says screens out returning customers.

Are voicemails charged on Local Services Ads?

They can be. Google says a call that goes to voicemail or an automated greeting during your business hours is treated as a connected call and is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message. Business hours are the hours on your Google Business Profile, and if your ad schedule differs, unanswered calls are only charged inside both.

Can a cleaning company offer a discount for Google reviews?

No. Google says offering incentives, such as free or discounted services, in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Ask every client for a review with Google’s review link or QR code instead.
