Why roofers lose Google LSA leads

Roofing jobs are high-ticket, so every lost LSA lead hurts. Yet roofing crews are among the hardest to reach: on a roof, on a ladder, or across town doing an inspection.

After a storm, LSA demand jumps across a wide area. Without a deliberate service area, roofers end up paying for leads far outside the neighborhoods they actually want to work. Homeowners comparing roofers also lean heavily on reviews, since they’re trusting someone with a big job.

Frequently asked questions

Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for roofers?

LSA puts roofers at the very top of Google with the Google Verified badge, and you pay per lead instead of per click. It pays off when you answer every lead fast, rate leads back to Google, and keep your service area tight. Those are the areas LSA Command handles.

How should roofers set their LSA service area?

Start with the cities and zip codes where your jobs are most profitable and where you can show up for inspections quickly. Proximity is an LSA ranking factor, so far-away areas usually rank lower. LSA Command’s Radius tool builds that list and pushes it to LSA, and the Rank Tracker shows where you appear in each city.
