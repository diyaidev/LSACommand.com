Why plumbers lose Google LSA leads

Plumbing leads are urgent. A homeowner with water on the floor calls the first two or three listings and books whoever answers. If your crew is under a sink and the call goes to voicemail, that job is gone in minutes.

Plumbers also see plenty of low-value LSA traffic: wrong numbers, sales calls, and spam. Paying for those leads and never rating them back to Google teaches the system nothing.

How to run Google Local Services Ads for a plumbing company in winter (2026)

Local Services Ads now run inside Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. For plumbers, winter means frozen and burst pipes, water heaters that quit on the coldest night of the year, and phones that ring hardest when the crew is already out. Here’s how to set LSA up for it, whether you run the account yourself or for clients.

What a plumber pays for

“Plumbers” is on Google’s list of Local Services categories in the US (Local Services Help). You pay per lead, not per click. Google says “Standard clicks on your ad are free,” and “Lead prices may vary depending on your location, job type, lead type, or bidding strategy.” (Google Ads Help) Google doesn’t publish an average price.

For a reference point, the best public number we’ve found is a vendor sample, not Google data: SearchLight Digital’s February 2026 benchmark of 888 home-service contractors put plumbing at $57 per lead across 230 accounts, and drain and sewer at $59 across 23 (SearchLight Digital). It’s one company’s sample from one winter month, so use it to sanity-check your own numbers, not to predict them.

Open 24 hours or not?

Plenty of plumbers list “open 24 hours” to catch emergency calls. Under Google’s charging rules, that listing decides which missed calls you pay for.

Google’s charged-leads page says a missed call is charged when it’s “during your business hours and the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds.” If a call goes to voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours, “it’s treated as a connected call and is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message.” And: “‘Business hours’ are the hours you set in your Google Business Profile.” If the ad schedule is different, unanswered calls are “only charged when they happen during your active ad schedule and within your business hours.” (Local Services Help)

Put together: if the profile says 24 hours and the 1 a.m. burst-pipe call rolls to voicemail, that can be a charged lead nobody spoke to. If someone really answers around the clock, 24 hours is worth listing, because emergency work is where a lot of winter revenue is. If not, list the hours someone really picks up.

Responsiveness also feeds ranking. Google says “Missed calls and slow response times can lower your ranking and visibility.” (Google Ads Help) More in our guide to LSA missed-call charges.

The third setup is the one most shops miss. Google says message and booking options “provide alternative channels for customers to reach you during nights, weekends, or busy hours,” and to opt in only “if you have the capacity to respond to customer inquiries within 24 hours.” (Google Ads Help) Google also says “Message leads are typically priced lower than phone leads, although that is not always the case.” (Google Ads Help)

Don’t try to game the daytime setup by running the ad all night against short profile hours. Your profile hours are what customers see, and Google says to “Ensure your ad schedule, business hours, and service offerings are always accurate.” (Google Ads Help) Pick the setup you can actually staff.

Freeze weeks and the budget

Since the move to Google Ads, Google says “Your historical average weekly budget is automatically divided by 7 to determine your daily average budget,” monthly spend “is capped at your daily average budget multiplied by 30.4,” and “Daily spend may fluctuate on high-demand days, but your monthly billing will not exceed this limit.” (Google Ads Help)

So a freeze week can use a big share of the month’s budget early. That’s the system working, not overspend. What to do:

  • Check pacing the Monday after any freeze. If you’re ahead and can staff more calls, raise the budget. If you can’t staff them, don’t: leads nobody answers turn straight back into missed calls.
  • Keep the service area to where your trucks will go. Google says to limit service areas “to regions you can reasonably service within a two-hour drive.” (Google Ads Help)
  • Don’t switch bid strategies over one surge. Google says “If your campaign receives fewer than 15 leads per month, keep it on the default Maximize Conversions strategy” (Google Ads Help), and Target CPA is for campaigns that consistently clear that. A freeze isn’t consistent. More in Maximize Conversions vs Target CPA for LSA and our LSA budget and bidding guide.

Hiring seasonal technicians

Winter is when plumbers add technicians. Google says “In specific business categories and locations, Local Services Ads require background checks for the business, the business owner, and their field worker roster.” In those categories, every employee, contractor or temporary worker “who will perform core services for the customer must complete a background check,” while office and customer-support staff who don’t service customers directly don’t. And “Businesses aren’t charged for the background check.” (Local Services Help)

Google’s page doesn’t name the trades, so check the account’s Local Services dashboard to see whether checks apply in your category and state [CONFIRM: whether background checks currently apply to plumbers in the US. Frequently asked questions

Should a plumber’s Local Services Ads profile say open 24 hours?

Only if someone really answers 24 hours. Google treats a call that reaches voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours as a connected call and charges it if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, and business hours are the hours on your Google Business Profile. If nobody answers at night, list your real hours and consider message leads for nights instead.

Are voicemails charged on Local Services Ads for plumbers?

They can be. A missed call during business hours is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, and a call that hits voicemail or a greeting during business hours is charged the same way, whether or not the caller leaves a message. Unanswered calls are only charged inside both the active ad schedule and the business hours.

Can a plumbing customer send a message at night instead of calling?

Yes, if the campaign is opted into message and booking goals. Google says these give customers a way to reach you during nights, weekends, or busy hours, and recommends opting in only if you can respond within 24 hours. Google also says message leads are typically priced lower than phone leads, though not always.

Do plumbers get a credit for LSA leads for work they don’t do?

Not any more. Google no longer supports credits for “job type not serviced” and “geo not serviced” leads, so turn off job types the company doesn’t do and keep the service area to where your trucks will go.

How much does a Local Services Ads lead cost for plumbers?

Google doesn’t publish an average. It says lead prices may vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding strategy. One vendor sample, SearchLight Digital’s February 2026 benchmark of 888 home-service contractors, put plumbing at $57 per lead across 230 accounts. Treat that as a reference point, not a quote for your market.
