How to run Google Local Services Ads for an HVAC company in winter (2026)

Local Services Ads now run inside Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. The rules are the same for every trade, but HVAC winters have their own shape: the expensive no-heat calls come at night and on weekends, one cold snap can swamp the phones for days, and it’s when shops add technicians. Here’s how to set LSA up for it, whether you run the account yourself or for clients.

What an HVAC company pays for

“HVAC (heating or air conditioning)” is on Google’s list of Local Services categories in the US (Local Services Help). You pay per lead, not per click: Google says “Standard clicks on your ad are free,” and “Lead prices may vary depending on your location, job type, lead type, or bidding strategy.” (Google Ads Help) Google doesn’t publish an average.

The best public reference point we’ve found is a vendor sample, not Google data. SearchLight Digital’s February 2026 benchmark of 888 home-service contractors put HVAC at $51 per lead across 409 accounts (SearchLight Digital). February is a winter month, which helps here, but it’s one company’s sample. Your market will be different, so use it as a sanity check, not a forecast.

The 24-hour decision

The most important winter setting for an HVAC company isn’t in the campaign. It’s your hours.

Google’s charged-leads page says a missed call is charged when it’s “during your business hours and the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds.” If a call goes to voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours, “it’s treated as a connected call and is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message.” And: “‘Business hours’ are the hours you set in your Google Business Profile.” If the ad schedule is different, unanswered calls are “only charged when they happen during your active ad schedule and within your business hours.” (Local Services Help)

So “open 24 hours” on the Business Profile is a promise. If someone really answers at 2 a.m., no-heat calls are where a lot of winter revenue is. If nobody does, every overnight voicemail inside those listed hours can be a charged lead nobody talked to.

It costs position too. Google says ranking is “based on your bid, responsiveness, and profile quality,” and “Missed calls and slow response times can lower your ranking and visibility.” (Google Ads Help) The full rules are in our guide to LSA missed-call charges.

Pick the one that matches who actually answers:

The third setup is the one most shops miss. Google says message and booking options “provide alternative channels for customers to reach you during nights, weekends, or busy hours,” and to opt in only “if you have the capacity to respond to customer inquiries within 24 hours.” (Google Ads Help) Google also says “Message leads are typically priced lower than phone leads, although that is not always the case.” (Google Ads Help)

Don’t try to game the daytime setup by running the ad all night against short profile hours. Your profile hours are what customers see, and Google says to “Ensure your ad schedule, business hours, and service offerings are always accurate.” (Google Ads Help) Pick the setup you can actually staff.

Cold snaps and the budget

Since the move to Google Ads, Google says “Your historical average weekly budget is automatically divided by 7 to determine your daily average budget,” monthly spend “is capped at your daily average budget multiplied by 30.4,” and “Daily spend may fluctuate on high-demand days, but your monthly billing will not exceed this limit.” (Google Ads Help)

So a freeze week can use a big share of the month’s budget early. That’s the system working, not overspend. What to do:

  • Check pacing the Monday after any freeze. If you’re ahead and can staff more calls, raise the budget. If you can’t staff them, don’t: leads nobody answers turn straight back into missed calls.
  • Keep the service area to where your trucks will go. Google says to limit service areas “to regions you can reasonably service within a two-hour drive.” (Google Ads Help)
  • Don’t switch bid strategies over one surge. Google says “If your campaign receives fewer than 15 leads per month, keep it on the default Maximize Conversions strategy” (Google Ads Help), and Target CPA is for campaigns that consistently clear that. A freeze isn’t consistent. More in Maximize Conversions vs Target CPA for LSA and our LSA budget and bidding guide.

Hiring seasonal technicians

Winter is when HVAC companies add technicians. Google says “In specific business categories and locations, Local Services Ads require background checks for the business, the business owner, and their field worker roster.” In those categories, every employee, contractor or temporary worker “who will perform core services for the customer must complete a background check,” while office and customer-support staff who don’t service customers directly don’t. And “Businesses aren’t charged for the background check.” (Local Services Help)

Google’s page doesn’t name the trades, so check the account’s Local Services dashboard to see whether checks apply in your category and state [CONFIRM: whether background checks currently apply to HVAC companies in the US. Frequently asked questions

Are overnight voicemails charged on Local Services Ads for HVAC companies?

They can be. If a call reaches voicemail or an automated greeting during your business hours, Google treats it as a connected call and charges it if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message. Business hours are the hours on your Google Business Profile, and unanswered calls are only charged inside both the active ad schedule and those hours. If the profile says open 24 hours, overnight voicemails count.

Can HVAC customers send a message at night instead of calling?

Yes, if the campaign is opted into message and booking goals. Google describes them as alternative channels for customers to reach you during nights, weekends, or busy hours, and recommends opting in only if you can respond within 24 hours.

Will a cold snap use up an HVAC company’s LSA budget?

It can use a big share of it early. Since the move to Google Ads, the old weekly budget is divided by 7 into a daily average budget. Daily spend may fluctuate on high-demand days, but monthly billing won’t exceed the daily average multiplied by 30.4. Check pacing after a freeze and only raise the budget if the team can answer more calls.

Do new HVAC technicians need an LSA background check?

In the business categories and locations where Google requires background checks, every worker who performs core services for customers must complete one; office and customer-support staff who don’t service customers directly don’t. Google doesn’t charge for the check. Look in the Local Services dashboard to see whether it applies to your account, and start it the week the tech is hired.

How much does a Local Services Ads lead cost for HVAC companies?

Google doesn’t publish an average. It says lead prices may vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding strategy. One vendor sample, SearchLight Digital’s February 2026 benchmark of 888 home-service contractors, put HVAC at $51 per lead across 409 accounts. Treat that as a reference point, not a quote for your market.
