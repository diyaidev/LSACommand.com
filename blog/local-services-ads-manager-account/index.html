How to Manage Client Local Services Ads From a Manager Account

Running LSA for clients out of a pile of separate logins works until about the fifth account. Here’s how to set up manager-account access properly, and what to settle with each client before Google moves LSA into Google Ads.

Most agencies start running Local Services Ads the same way: the client adds someone’s Gmail address to their account, and that person logs in and out of a dozen accounts. It works until a team member leaves, a client’s card is declined and nobody sees the alert, or two people change the same budget in the same week.

A Google Ads manager account fixes most of that. It also matters more now, because Google is moving LSA into Google Ads, where manager accounts are already the standard way agencies work.

What a manager account is (and isn’t)

A manager account, often still called an MCC, is a Google Ads account that lets you view and manage multiple Google Ads accounts from one sign-in. It’s a separate account you create, not an upgrade of an existing one. Google also caps the number of Google Ads accounts associated with a single email address at 20, and suggests a manager account if you manage more than that. Source: Google Ads Help

For LSA specifically, Google says manager accounts are set up and edited in Google Ads; they can’t be created or changed from inside Local Services Ads. Linking an LSA account to a manager account is also required for features like consolidated monthly billing. Source: Google Local Services Help

What a manager link doesn’t do: it doesn’t make you the owner of the client’s account. Google treats ownership as a separate grant the client’s admin has to make. More on why that matters below.

How to link a client’s LSA account

Linking needs two sides: a request from your manager account and an acceptance from the client’s account.

  1. Get the client’s customer ID. Every LSA account has a Google Ads customer ID, the 10-digit number shown in the account.
  2. Send the link request. In your manager account, go to Sub-account settings, click the plus button, choose Link existing account, enter the customer ID (one per line if you’re linking several), preview, then send. Source: Google Ads Help
  3. Have the client accept. Users on the client account get an email invitation. They can also accept from Admin › Access and security › Managers, under the link request.
  4. Confirm. Once the client accepts, you get a confirmation email and the account appears under your manager account.

Google notes that linking an existing account doesn’t change its history or the original advertiser’s access, and the client can unlink at any time. Pending requests can be withdrawn from the Sent link requests view. Source: Google Ads Help

Pick the right access level for each person

LSA has five account access levels, and only one of them can change bids and budget. Per Google’s help center:

Access levelWhat it allows
Email-onlyReceives reports and notification emails
Billing-onlyViews and edits billing information
Read-onlyViews leads, profile, settings and users; can reply to lead notification emails and messages
StandardEverything in Read-only plus billing, editing the business profile, replying to leads in the account and adding email-only users
AdministrativeEverything, including editing bidding and budget and managing users and access

Users with administrative access to a manager account can manage any part of the linked account and its campaigns. Source: Google Local Services Help

A sensible default for agencies:

  • Your agency: administrative access through the manager account, so budget and bid changes go through you.
  • The owner: administrative access on their own account. It’s their business.
  • The client’s office or intake staff: Standard, so they can work leads but not budgets.
  • The bookkeeper: Billing-only.
  • Anyone who just needs the numbers: Email-only.

Billing access is controlled separately in the Google payments profile, so make sure someone on your team can see payment alerts. A declined card is one of the most common reasons an account quietly stops; see Local Services Ads not showing.

Decide who owns the account, in writing

This is the conversation agencies avoid and then regret.

Some agencies create and own the LSA account themselves. The argument: you built it, you optimized it, and the client can’t walk away with your work. Others insist the client owns the account and the payment profile, and the agency is a linked manager. The argument: it’s the client’s business identity, the verification is tied to the owner and their Business Profile, and the client trusts you more because they can leave.

Either model can work. What doesn’t work is leaving it undecided. Put these in the contract:

  • Who owns the LSA account and the Google Ads account it lives in.
  • Whose payment method pays for leads, and who sees billing alerts.
  • What happens to access, reporting and history if the relationship ends.
  • Who is responsible for verification steps only the owner can complete. Our verification checklist lists them.

Get ready for the move into Google Ads

Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as a Performance Max campaign type with a pay-per-lead goal. Search Engine Journal reported in July 2026 that the transition would begin in August with a limited group of US advertisers, with more US advertisers later in 2026 and remaining categories and non-US accounts in 2027. Accounts move in phases, and each account’s administrator is notified before that account moves. Source: Search Engine Journal

For an agency with a portfolio, that means a rolling migration across your client list. Things reported so far that affect how you manage accounts: Source: PPC Land

  • Notice goes to the account administrator, reportedly 14 days before the migration date, with a reminder and a confirmation. If the client is the only admin, you may not see it. Ask clients to forward anything from Google about LSA.
  • Historical reporting doesn’t carry over. Past impressions, clicks, spend and ad-level breakdowns stay behind, and the old dashboard becomes unreachable. Export every client’s history before their date.
  • Lead history does transfer, reportedly into a Lead Manager view inside Google Ads.
  • Manual max-per-lead bidding goes away, replaced by a single campaign-level target cost per lead. Clients with very different targets by trade may need separate campaigns.
  • Weekly budgets become daily, with the monthly cap at the daily amount multiplied by 30.4.

Run the portfolio, not just the accounts

Once every client is linked under one manager account, a few habits keep the portfolio clean:

  • Name accounts consistently so the right person changes the right account.
  • Keep a migration tracker with each client’s notice date, export date and post-migration check.
  • Review access every quarter. Remove former staff on both sides.
  • Watch every account weekly for zero-impression weeks, verification requests and billing alerts.
  • Report on outcomes, not just leads. A manager account shows spend and leads; it doesn’t tell the client which leads were answered, rated, credited or booked. Our guide to LSA reporting software for agencies covers what a useful report needs.

Frequently asked questions

Can you link Local Services Ads to a Google Ads manager account?

Yes. Every LSA account has a Google Ads customer ID. Send a link request from your manager account (Sub-account settings › Link existing account), and the client accepts it from Admin › Access and security › Managers.

Do I create the manager account inside Local Services Ads?

No. Google says manager accounts for LSA are set up and edited in Google Ads, not in Local Services Ads.

Does linking a client’s LSA account give my agency ownership?

No. Linking gives your manager account access; ownership is a separate grant the client’s admin has to make. Agree on ownership, billing and what happens at offboarding in your contract.

Which access level lets someone change the LSA budget?

Only Administrative access can edit bidding and budget and manage users. Standard users can work leads and edit the profile but can’t change budget.

What should agencies do before an LSA account migrates into Google Ads?

Export each account’s reporting history, because it doesn’t carry over. Make sure Google’s migration notices reach your team, and recheck budgets and bid targets after the move.
