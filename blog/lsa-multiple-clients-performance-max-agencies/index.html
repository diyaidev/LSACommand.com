Managing LSA for Multiple Clients After Performance Max (Agency Setup)

Since Local Services Ads moved into Performance Max, every user can get every client’s lead emails by default, call recordings last only 30 days, and a Business Profile edit can pause a campaign for two days. Here’s the agency setup that handles all of it, plus a five-point weekly check per client.

This guide is for agency owners running Google Local Services Ads for a book of clients: fifteen, thirty, fifty accounts, where one missed setting gets copied across every client you manage.

The move into Performance Max changed more than the dashboard. Every user at your agency can now get every client’s lead emails by default. Call recordings are kept for only 30 days. And a change someone makes on a client’s Business Profile can pause their LSA campaign for two days without anyone touching the ads. Below is the setup that handles all of it, plus a five-point weekly check you can run per client.

If you haven’t migrated yet, start with the agency migration playbook (what to save, what changes on day one, and the reporting bug). This page is what comes after: running LSA across many clients inside Google Ads, week after week.

1. One manager account, and where new clients start

Everything lives in a Google Ads manager account. Even before the migration, Google’s Local Services help center said it plainly: “Local Services Ads use Google Ads to set up manager accounts.” So the structure is one agency manager account with every client’s account linked underneath it. Source: Local Services Help

Linking matters for money, too. Google says linking an LSA account to a manager account “is required for features like consolidated monthly billing.” If you bill clients for ad spend, or want one invoice, this is where it starts.

Here’s the catch for new clients. Google says you can’t create new campaigns with pay-per-lead goals directly inside Google Ads at this time; new LSA accounts still start at g.co/localservices. Source: Google Ads Help So your onboarding checklist has two halves: the client signs up through the LSA flow, then the account gets linked into your manager account. Write both down, because a new hire will only do one of them. Our guide to managing client LSA from a manager account covers the linking steps.

2. Access levels, the admin, and who owns the account

For every client, know who the account administrator is. Google sends migration notices to the administrator, and only the administrator can manage everyone else’s access. Then give each person the lowest level that lets them do the job. Google’s LSA access table has five levels, from email-only up to administrative, and spells out what each one can do, from receiving notification emails to editing bidding and budget to managing users. Source: Local Services Help A simple agency mapping:

Access levelTypical agency role
AdministrativeAgency owner or ops lead (the only level that manages users and access)
StandardAccount manager doing day-to-day work
Read-onlyReporting or QA
Email-onlyA client contact who only needs alerts
Billing-onlyBookkeeper

A junior team member answering message leads doesn’t need admin. And put ownership in your client agreement on day one: who owns the account, and what happens to it if the client leaves. Some agencies keep it and some clients insist on owning it. Either can work if it’s in writing.

3. Lead notifications are now on for everyone

This one surprises agencies. After migration, lead emails are controlled by a notifications toggle in Google Ads, and Google says: “All account users and direct managers are automatically opted in to receive these email alerts by default, regardless of any previous notification preferences configured in the Local Services Ads dashboard.” Source: Google Ads Help

If your team sits on the manager account, everyone can start getting every lead email for every client. Twenty clients and five team members is a firehose, and the usual result is that people start ignoring lead emails. That’s the worst outcome in a product that ranks you partly on how fast you respond.

Google also requires at least one user per account to stay opted in. So decide it on purpose. For every client: one named owner who gets the lead alerts, a shared inbox as the backup, everyone else switched off. Then send a test lead and confirm the alert actually lands. 4. Routing numbers and message delivery

This part is a real upgrade. In Google Ads you can edit the phone number your call and message leads route to, and Google says the change happens in real time. In the campaign’s contact settings you also choose how message leads are delivered: email, text message, or both. Source: Google Ads Help

Why it matters: Google now charges a missed call during business hours when the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds, and a call that reaches voicemail counts as connected. (Full detail in Google now charges for missed calls.) The routing number decides whether a paid lead reaches a human. For every client, check three things:

  • Does the number ring a person, or roll to something that actually answers?
  • Are message leads going to an inbox or phone someone watches? See how to respond to LSA message leads.
  • Does anyone respond within minutes, not hours?

5. Call recordings disappear after 30 days

Most agencies haven’t noticed this one. Google’s leads page says, word for word: “Call recordings and sensitive conversation history are stored in the Lead Manager for up to 30 days due to data privacy policies.” Source: Google Ads Help

Think about where you use recordings: training a client’s front desk, checking whether a lead was real, showing a client that the phone rang and nobody picked up, or settling an argument two months later about lead quality. After day 30 they’re gone.

So add a weekly archive step. The Leads page (Goals, then Conversions, then Leads) is where Google says you “check, download, or interact with” your leads. Download every client’s leads each week and save the recordings you’d want for training or proof. If you sync leads into a CRM, our LSA CRM integration guide covers what to check.

6. The Business Profile tripwire

After migration, core business details sync one way, from the client’s Google Business Profile into Google Ads. Google says hours, phone number and minor name changes sync within 24 hours. But “significant updates to your business name, storefront address, or primary category require verification,” and the campaign “may temporarily pause” while Google reviews them, which “typically takes 24 to 48 hours.” Source: Google Ads Help

Here’s how that goes wrong at an agency. Your SEO team, or the client’s nephew, changes the primary category on the Business Profile to chase map rankings. Nobody tells the LSA side. The campaign pauses for two days, leads stop, and nobody on the ads side touched anything.

There’s a second trap. Profile hours sync over, and missed-call charges apply inside business hours. Change the profile hours and you’ve changed the window in which a missed call costs your client money. The SEO team just edited the billing window.

The fix is one rule: any change to a client’s business name, address, primary category or hours goes through one person and gets logged with a date. If leads drop, that log is the first thing you check.

7. Check what Google writes for your clients

Google says that if a business website is assigned to the campaign, “automated assets (including AI-generated callouts) will be automatically enabled.” You can opt out by turning off “Asset optimization” in campaign settings or by removing the website. Source: Google Ads Help

Often that’s fine. But for a client in a regulated field, like a law firm, or a client who is particular about how they’re described, someone should look at what’s showing before the client finds it. Decide per client: on and reviewed monthly, or off. Google also retired BBB callouts and asks for at least six other structured callouts in the Assets tab.

Frequently asked questions

How do agencies manage Local Services Ads for multiple clients?

Through a Google Ads manager account, with each client’s account linked underneath it. Google says LSA uses Google Ads to set up manager accounts, and linking is required for features like consolidated monthly billing.

Can I create a new LSA campaign inside Google Ads?

Not at this time. Google says new campaigns with pay-per-lead goals can’t be created directly in Google Ads; new Local Services accounts still start at g.co/localservices and can then be linked to your manager account.

Who gets LSA lead email notifications after the move to Performance Max?

All account users and direct managers are opted in by default, regardless of earlier settings. Individual users can turn them off, but each account must keep at least one user opted in.

How long does Google keep LSA call recordings?

Google says call recordings and sensitive conversation history are stored in the Lead Manager for up to 30 days due to data privacy policies. Download or archive anything you need before then.

Can a Google Business Profile change pause a Local Services campaign?

Yes. Significant changes to the business name, storefront address or primary category trigger a verification review that typically takes 24 to 48 hours, and Google says the campaign may temporarily pause during it.
