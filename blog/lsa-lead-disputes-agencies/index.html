How LSA lead credits work now

The old manual “dispute this lead” flow is gone for most advertisers. Google now runs automated lead credits:

  • Some bad leads are never charged. Google says leads are assessed when the customer first makes contact, and leads it judges invalid or low quality aren’t charged. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Charged leads get re-checked. Google’s models reassess charged leads over time and may credit them automatically if they later look low quality. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Credits land later, and the charge stays on the invoice. Google says credits are applied to the account balance within 30 days in most cases, and the original charge still appears on the invoice. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Feedback is how you tell Google. Advertisers rate a lead through the lead feedback survey on the Leads page. Google says it “may also occasionally credit” leads reported as poor quality there. Source: Google Local Services Help

In other words, your job has changed. It’s no longer arguing case by case. It’s making sure every lead in every account gets rated accurately, and checking what Google actually credited.

Set client expectations early, because a lot of “bad” leads aren’t creditable:

The fix for wrong-job and wrong-area leads isn’t a dispute. It’s tighter settings. Google’s own advice for unwanted leads is to revisit service areas, job types and categories. Source: Google Local Services Help See our service area targeting page.

The rule that can get a client suspended

When you’re rating hundreds of leads, it’s tempting to mark every lead the client didn’t like as bad. Don’t. Google’s LSA requirements list “attempting to trick our systems or support channels in an attempt to get credits for valid leads” as a circumvention violation. Source: Google Local Services Help The same section covers diverting customers to another number to avoid paying for a lead.

A suspension doesn’t only hurt LSA. Google says any suspension of the Google Ads account also suspends Local Services Ads. Source: Google Local Services Help So write a short rating policy for your team, and apply it the same way in every account:

  • Rate on what happened in the call or message, not on whether the job closed.
  • A real customer asking for a service the client doesn’t offer is a settings issue. Note it, fix the job types, and don’t expect a credit.
  • A real customer who didn’t book is a sales issue, not a bad lead.
  • Keep the recording or message reference for anything you rate as spam or invalid, so a second person can check it.

A weekly lead-feedback routine for a client book

The routine below works whether you have five accounts or fifty. Credits arrive on a 30-day cycle, so weekly is frequent enough and still manageable.

  1. Pull every lead from every account. Use your manager account or the API, not each client’s login. See our manager account guide.
  2. Review calls and messages. Listen to recordings or read the thread. Google notes call recordings aren’t available for tax specialists or US health care verticals, so those accounts need notes from the client. Source: Google Local Services Help
  3. Rate every lead. Submit lead feedback for each one, good and bad. Google uses the feedback to learn which leads each business wants. Source: Google Local Services Help At scale, feedback can also be submitted through the Google Ads API. Source: Google Ads API docs
  4. Fix the cause. For each pattern of wrong-job or wrong-area leads, adjust job types or service areas that week.
  5. Check charge status. Each lead shows Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited on the Leads page. Source: Google Local Services Help Log status changes so you know what Google actually credited.
  6. Escalate the rare exception. If something looks wrong beyond what the feedback survey covers, contact Google support with the lead details. Don’t resubmit the same lead hoping for a different answer.

What changes after the move to Performance Max

Google is moving LSA into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. The Leads page in Google Ads replaces the old LSA lead inbox, and Google says it keeps the charged status, lead type and a lead feedback form. Source: Google Ads Help

Two things to plan for across your book:

  • Old performance reports don’t carry over. Google says historical LSA performance reports won’t move to Google Ads, though past leads and recordings will. Save each client’s credit history before their account migrates. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Accounts migrate on different dates. Google is phasing the move from August 2026 through 2027. Source: Google Ads Help For a while, some clients will be in the old dashboard and some in Google Ads, so your routine has to work in both places. Our agency migration playbook covers the rest.

How to report credits to clients

Clients remember the spam call, not the credit three weeks later. Report three numbers separately every month, and never blend them:

  • Leads you flagged. Your team’s assessment.
  • Leads rated to Google. Feedback actually submitted.
  • Credits Google recorded. Only what shows as Credited or as uncharged.

Only the third number is money back. Reporting “we disputed $400” when Google credited $90 is how agencies lose trust. Also explain that a credited lead still appears on the invoice as a charge. Clients who read invoices will ask. Source: Google Local Services Help Our LSA client report template has a section laid out this way.

Frequently asked questions

Can agencies still dispute Google LSA leads manually?

For most advertisers, no. Google now assesses leads automatically, credits ones it decides are low quality, and asks advertisers to rate leads through the lead feedback survey. Google says it may also occasionally credit leads reported as poor quality through that survey.

How long do LSA lead credits take?

Google says credits are applied to the account balance within 30 days in most cases. The original lead charge still appears on the invoice, so explain that to clients who read their bills.

Which LSA leads don’t qualify for credits?

Google no longer credits “job type not serviced” or “geo not serviced” leads, and lead credits aren’t available for health care verticals, tax specialists or advertisers in EMEA. Missed calls during business hours where the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds also count as valid leads.

Can rating too many leads as bad cause problems?

Yes. Google’s LSA requirements list attempting to trick its systems or support channels to get credits for valid leads as a policy violation, and a Google Ads suspension also suspends Local Services Ads. Rate leads on what actually happened.

Does lead feedback still work after LSA moves to Performance Max?

Google says the Leads page in Google Ads replaces the LSA lead inbox and includes a lead feedback form and charge status. Historical LSA performance reports don’t carry over, so save each client’s credit history before migration.
