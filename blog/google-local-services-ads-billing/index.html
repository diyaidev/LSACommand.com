Google Local Services Ads Billing: How It Works and How Agencies Should Set It Up

LSA billing runs on Google Ads, which surprises clients who never touched Google Ads. Here’s how charges and payment thresholds work, how credits show up, what changes after the move to Performance Max, and the three ways agencies set billing up for clients.

Most home-service owners sign up for Local Services Ads (LSA) without ever having used Google Ads. Then the first charge arrives from “Google Ads,” at an odd amount, on an odd date, and the agency gets the call.

LSA billing is simple once you see how it works. Agencies still need a deliberate setup for every client: who pays, whose card is on file, and how ad spend is shown next to the agency’s fee. This guide covers both. It reflects Google’s help pages as of November 2, 2026.

How LSA billing works

Google says LSA “use Google Ads to power its billing and payment systems.” If the business already has a Google Ads account, LSA reuses those payment settings. If not, you add a credit card or bank account at sign-up and charges run automatically. Source: Google Local Services Help

Billing information is shared between the LSA and Google Ads accounts. Google says changes to billing settings affect both. Source: Google Local Services Help

The payment threshold

With automatic payments, Google charges the account in one of two cases, whichever comes first: Source: Google Local Services Help

  1. 30 days after the last payment
  2. when the balance reaches the payment threshold

The starting threshold depends on the account, country and currency. Each time it’s hit within a 30-day billing period, it rises in steps up to a set maximum. Google’s example:

StepWhat happens in Google’s example
Starting threshold$50 USD
Account reaches $75 in chargesThe card is charged and the threshold rises to $200
Balance hits $200 within 30 daysCharged again; threshold rises to $350
One more chargeThreshold rises to the $500 maximum

Source: Google Local Services Help, how to pay for LSA.

That’s why a new client sees several small charges in the first month and fewer, larger ones after. It isn’t double billing. In the old dashboard, the current balance and threshold are under Billing in the lead inbox menu.

What Google charges for

LSA charges per valid lead, not per click. Lead prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode. Source: Google Ads Help According to Google, a valid lead includes:

  • An answered call, or a call back where you speak with the customer.
  • A missed call during business hours where the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. If a phone menu asks the caller to press a key, the 20 seconds start after the first key press.
  • A text, email or voicemail from the customer. Google says message leads are typically priced lower than phone leads, though not always.
  • A booking request (US and Canada only).

Two rules save clients money. First, follow-up calls or messages from the same number or email within 15 days aren’t charged again. Second, “business hours” are the hours on the Google Business Profile, so wrong hours there can mean charged missed calls. Source: Google Ads Help For more on the 20-second rule, see LSA missed-call charges.

Your budget caps the bill

In the original LSA dashboard you set an average weekly budget, and Google calculates a monthly max: “average weekly budget x 30.4 ÷ 7.” Once the monthly max is hit, the ad stops showing for the rest of the month unless the budget changes. Source: Google Local Services Help

After the move to Google Ads, the weekly budget becomes a daily average budget (weekly divided by 7). Google says monthly spend is capped at the daily average budget multiplied by 30.4, so “your monthly billing will not exceed this limit,” even if daily spend swings. Source: Google Ads Help

ILLUSTRATION: a client with a $350 weekly budget has a monthly max of $350 × 30.4 ÷ 7 = $1,520. After migration that becomes a $50 daily average budget, and $50 × 30.4 = $1,520 again. The cap doesn’t change, only how it’s shown.

Credits, charge statuses and the invoice

Google screens leads when they arrive and doesn’t charge invalid or low-quality ones. Charged leads are reassessed over time and can be credited automatically. Source: Google Local Services Help Three points matter for billing:

  • Credits land within about 30 days. Google says credits are usually applied to the account balance within 30 days, and “the original lead charge will still appear on your invoice.” Clients who compare the invoice to their lead list need to know this.
  • Every lead has a charge status: Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited. After migration these show in the Charge Status column on the Leads page in Google Ads. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Some leads can’t be credited. Google says lead credits aren’t available for health care verticals, tax specialists or advertisers in EMEA. It no longer gives credits for “job type not serviced” or “geo not serviced” leads, so service areas and job types have to be right. Source: Google Local Services Help

Google says the lead feedback survey helps it learn which leads you want, and that it may “occasionally” credit leads reported as poor quality. Source: Google Ads Help That’s why rating every lead is worth the time. Our lead disputes and credits page covers the process.

Three ways agencies set up client billing

This is the agency decision. Each option works; pick one per client and write it into the agreement.

About monthly invoicing

Google describes monthly invoicing as a setting typically used by large advertisers and by agencies managing clients’ accounts. Here’s what Google lists: Source: Google Ads Help

  • Requirements include:
    • the account is linked to a manager account
    • the business has been registered for at least a year
    • the Google Ads account has been active and in good standing for six months
    • spend of at least $5,000 USD a month in any 3 of the last 12 months
  • Terms: Google reviews credit history. If you’re approved, you get payment terms (typically 30 days) and a credit line.
  • Invoices: they arrive by email by the 5th business day of the month. Ads can slow or stop if you exceed the terms or the credit line.

From a manager account you can attach the same billing setup to several client accounts and receive one consolidated monthly invoice. You can also give each client its own billing setup to keep invoices separate. Source: Google Ads Help

Stay transparent about spend

Whichever setup you use, Google’s third-party policy asks agencies to be “transparent and honest” about their services and the costs of those services. Google requires a separate account for each end advertiser. Source: Google Advertising Policies Help In practice, show the client their Google charges separately from your management fee every month. Billing problems that stop the ads

Billing is one of the most common reasons a client’s ads go dark:

  • Interrupted billing. Google says interruptions in billing “may prevent your ad from appearing.” Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Google Ads suspension. “Any suspension in your Google Ads account will also suspend your Local Services Ads.” Source: Google Ads Help
  • A card that won’t add. Google suggests these steps: Source: Google Local Services Help
    • 1. Remove and re-add the payment method.
    • 2. Clear the cache.
    • 3. Try an incognito window or another browser.
    • 4. Ask the bank to accept Google Ads charges.
  • Partner affiliate accounts. If a client advertises through a partner affiliate such as a directory network, Google says those providers get leads through the partner’s platform, not directly from Google, and questions about those ads go to the partner. Source: Google Local Services Help

An agency billing checklist

  1. Decide who pays before sign-up and put it in the agreement.
  2. Check the payments profile belongs to the right legal entity. Under the manager account, choosing the payments profile is choosing “who will be financially responsible” for the account. Source: Google Ads Help
  3. Set the budget from the client’s monthly ceiling, using the 30.4 math above.
  4. Fix Business Profile hours so missed calls outside real hours aren’t charged.
  5. Rate every lead and review charge statuses each week.
  6. Reconcile monthly: invoice total, credits applied, and charged leads against booked jobs.
  7. Watch billing emails for declines, so a failed card doesn’t quietly stop a client’s leads.

Frequently asked questions

How does Google Local Services Ads billing work?

LSA uses Google Ads for billing. With automatic payments, Google charges the payment method either 30 days after the last payment or when the balance reaches the payment threshold, whichever comes first. You’re charged for valid leads, not clicks.

What is the Local Services Ads payment threshold?

It’s the balance that triggers a charge before the 30 days are up. Google sets the starting threshold by account, country and currency and raises it in steps when it’s hit within a billing period, up to a set maximum. In Google’s US example it starts at $50 and rises to a $500 maximum.

Why does a credited lead still show on my invoice?

Google says credits are usually applied to the account balance within 30 days, but the original lead charge still appears on the invoice. Check the charge status on each lead to see which ones were credited.

Can an agency pay for a client’s Local Services Ads?

Yes. An agency can put its own payment method on a client account or use monthly invoicing from its manager account. Charges still belong to the client’s account, so agencies need to show clients what they spent on ads separately from their fees.

Where is billing after Local Services Ads moves to Google Ads?

Google says LSA billing information is shared with Google Ads, so billing is managed in the Google Ads account. After the move, leads and their charge statuses are on the Leads page in Google Ads.
