Google Moved LSA Into Performance Max: What Agencies Must Do Now

Local Services Ads are changing. Google is moving every LSA account into Google Ads, the notice email may not reach you, and some reporting tools can show a migrated client at zero leads when nothing is wrong. Here’s the agency playbook: what changes, a six-step runbook for your whole client book, and how to turn the change into new clients.

This guide is for agency owners: SEO shops, PPC agencies, anyone managing Local Services Ads for a book of local clients. It’s written for the person responsible for twenty accounts, not one.

What changed, and when

Google is moving Local Services Ads out of the standalone LSA dashboard and into Google Ads, as a special kind of Performance Max campaign with pay-per-lead goals. LSA is not dead. You still pay per lead, not per click. You still show at the top of Search and on Maps, and the Google Verified badge carries over. What changes is how you manage it, report on it and bid on it.

Google’s published timeline: Source: Google Ads Help

WaveWho moves
August 2026Select US home and storefront service advertisers: plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, moving
Late 2026Broader groups, including service-area businesses without a storefront and accounts with custom bidding or booking setups
2027Non-US accounts and all remaining categories

If most of your clients are service-area businesses that haven’t moved yet, they’re in the late-2026 wave. That’s now.

Six changes that matter to an agency on migration day

1. The notice goes to the account administrator

Google emails the administrator listed on the profile 14 days before migration, sends a final reminder 7 days later, and emails again once it’s done. If your client set up the account with their own email, the warning lands in an inbox you don’t watch.

2. Performance history doesn’t move

Past impressions, clicks, weekly spend and ad-level reports do not migrate. Lead history does carry over: contact details, message threads and older call recordings move to the new Leads page. The numbers you use to prove your value stay behind unless you save them first.

3. Weekly budgets become a daily average

Google divides the historical average weekly budget by 7, and caps monthly spend at that daily figure times 30.4. A $2,100 weekly budget becomes $300 a day, with a monthly cap of about $9,120. If you don’t explain it first, the new number looks like a change you made.

4. Manual bidding is gone

Manual bidding and manual max cost-per-lead aren’t supported. If a client had different targets for different categories in one campaign, Google merges them into one campaign-level target. The only way to keep separate targets is to split each category into its own campaign.

5. BBB callouts are out

Google no longer supports BBB callouts. It asks advertisers to select at least six other structured callouts in the campaign’s assets tab, such as business hours, specialties or accepted payment methods.

6. Allow two weeks to settle

Google says to allow up to two weeks for the migration to complete and performance to ramp back to stable levels. Expect a client call in those two weeks.

The agency runbook: six steps across your client book

Step 1: Inventory every account

For each client, record the category, whether they’re a storefront or service-area business, which wave that puts them in, whether they’ve migrated, and the most important column: who the account administrator is. If it isn’t an email you monitor, get added or set up forwarding. Our guide to managing multiple client accounts covers the access side.

Step 2: Archive the history

For every account that hasn’t migrated, download the performance reports now. Pull at least twelve months, by month: leads, charged versus credited leads, cost per lead, spend, and the budget and settings behind them. Google says a dedicated download page is coming, and suggests screenshots if you need reports immediately. Without it you can’t show a client you made things better, and they can’t tell whether the migration made things worse.

Step 3: Rebaseline budgets before the client sees them

Run the weekly ÷ 7 math for every client and send a one-line note: “Your budget is now shown as a daily average. Same spend, different display.” It’s a two-minute email that prevents a cancellation.

Step 4: Decide on splits for multi-category clients

If a client runs, say, plumbing and water heaters with very different lead values, decide before migration whether they need separate campaigns to keep separate targets.

Step 5: Fix the callouts

Replace the BBB callout with six structured callouts. Quick, and an easy line in your client report.

Step 6: Run a 14-day watch after migration

Check lead flow every day. Confirm the lead routing number, which you can now edit directly in Google Ads. And reset notifications: lead email alerts are now controlled under Admin › Notifications in Google Ads, and Google opts every account user and direct manager in by default, regardless of what was set in the old dashboard. Check who is actually receiving them. Even before the migration, [we found that roughly 1 in 10 businesses we onboard weren’t receiving their LSA notifications at all]. A move like this is exactly when that breaks. More on LSA lead notifications.

The silent failure: reports that show zero leads

A migrated account doesn’t show up in the Google Ads API as a Local Services campaign anymore. It shows up as Performance Max with local services enabled. Any dashboard, reporting tool or script that pulls “Local Services campaigns” can quietly drop that client to zero leads. The leads are still coming in; your report just stopped counting them.

Picture the conversation: the client opens your monthly report, sees zero leads, and you find out from them.

There’s a second change. Google removed the lead’s email address (LocalServicesLead.ContactDetails.email) from the Google Ads API in version 25. If anything you’ve built depends on that field, check it. Source: Google Ads API release notes

For every migrated client, compare the leads in your report against the Leads page in Google Ads for the same week. If they don’t match, your tool hasn’t caught up. Ask your vendor directly: “Do you handle Performance Max campaigns with local services enabled?” Our LSA reporting software checklist and CRM integration guide cover what else to test.

Turn the change into new clients

Every business running LSA is about to have a confusing month: a new dashboard, budgets that look different, lost history, and, since October 1, missed calls that can be billed as leads. That’s the best opening an agency gets all year.

The offer to lead with is a free LSA migration and missed-call check with three findings: did they save their history, how many missed calls were they charged for in October, and how many message leads went unanswered. It takes about 15 minutes, and most accounts have at least one real problem.

Frequently asked questions

Who gets Google’s notice before an LSA account migrates to Performance Max?

The account administrator listed on the profile, 14 days before the migration date, with a final reminder 7 days later. If the client is the only admin, the agency may never see it.

Does LSA reporting history carry over to Google Ads?

No. Performance reports such as impressions, clicks, weekly spend and ad-level reports do not migrate. Lead history does transfer to the new Leads page. Save performance data before each account moves.

How does the LSA weekly budget convert after migration?

Google divides the weekly budget by 7 for a daily average and caps monthly spend at that figure times 30.4: $2,100 a week becomes $300 a day, capped at about $9,120 a month.

Can I still set different bids for different LSA categories?

Not inside one campaign. Manual bidding is gone and Google merges category targets into one campaign-level target. Split categories into separate campaigns to keep separate targets.

Why does my reporting tool show zero leads for a migrated LSA client?

Migrated accounts appear in the Google Ads API as Performance Max with local services enabled, not as Local Services campaigns, so tools that only pull the old type can drop to zero. Compare your report with the Google Ads Leads page for the same week.
