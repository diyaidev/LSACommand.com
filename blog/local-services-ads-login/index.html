You go to log in to your Local Services Ads dashboard and land in Google Ads instead. No lead inbox, no weekly budget, nothing where it used to be. That isn’t a glitch. Local Services Ads now run as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, and once an account moves, the old dashboard address sends you to Google Ads for good.

This page is the map for after the move: where your leads are, where each setting went, what you still change on your Google Business Profile, how to read the new campaign screen, and what happened to last year’s reports. Everything here comes from Google’s own help pages, linked in each section. Google Ads menus move, so if a label on your screen differs, the help page is the tiebreaker.

What happened to the dashboard

Google is moving every Local Services Ads account into Google Ads. The ads themselves don’t change: Google says they keep showing only on Google Search and Google Maps in the same positions, you still pay per lead rather than per click, and there are still no keywords. What changes is where you manage them.

On that point Google is direct: after an account migrates, “you will no longer be able to access your original Local Services Ads dashboard,” and trying to log in on or after migration day “will automatically redirect you to your campaign overview in Google Ads.” Source: Google Ads Help

Google also warns first. The account administrator listed on the profile gets an email 14 days before the migration date, along with banners in the old dashboard, a reminder email 7 days later, and another email once the move is done. If nobody on your side saw those, they probably went to whoever is listed as the administrator. Google also says to allow up to two weeks after migration for the process to fully complete and for performance to settle.

Accounts move in phases, per Google’s timeline:

WhenWho moves
August 2026Select home and storefront service advertisers in the US, including plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control and moving.
Late 2026Broader groups, including service-area businesses without physical storefronts and accounts with custom bidding or booking setups.
2027Non-US accounts and all remaining categories not migrated in 2026.

Logging in: which account to pick

Use the same Google login that had access to the old dashboard, and go to Google Ads. If that login can see more than one Google Ads account, Google Ads asks you to choose one. Pick the account your Local Services campaign moved into. If confirmed, say “pick the account with the business’s name on it.”] If you’re not sure which one it is, your agency or Google’s migration support line (below) can tell you.

If you work with an agency, you may also see its manager account in the list. That’s normal: agencies manage client accounts through a Google Ads manager account.

And if your login gets you nowhere new, your account may simply not have migrated yet. Until your date, the old dashboard keeps working.

Where your leads are now

This is the question everyone asks first. Google says: open the Goals dropdown, click Conversions, and select Leads. Google says this table “replaces your Local Services Ads lead inbox” and has the same features: charged status, lead type (call, message and so on), a lead feedback form, and a download option so you can import leads into your own CRM. Source: Google Ads Help

  • Your old leads came with you. Google says past lead history, including customer contact details, back-and-forth message history and older call recordings, transfers to Google Ads and stays on the Leads page.
  • Message leads are here too. If message goals are on, click a lead to open the conversation panel. Google says you can read and reply to texts and emails from inside Google Ads. Our guide to responding to LSA message leads covers making sure those replies actually reach the customer.
  • Keep rating leads. The feedback form is still there, and it’s still how you tell Google which leads were good and which weren’t.

Budget, bidding, photos and pausing

Budget, bidding, locations and service types are under the Campaigns menu, then Settings. Two things will look different:

  • Weekly budget is now a daily average. Google divided your historical average weekly budget by 7. Monthly spend is capped at that daily average times 30.4, so a busy day can spend more than the daily number without the month going over.
  • Manual bidding is gone. Google says manual bidding, such as setting a maximum cost per lead, is no longer supported, and per-category Target CPA is replaced with one campaign-level Target CPA. If you need separate bids per category, Google’s answer is separate campaigns.

Photos and callouts are under Campaigns, then Assets. Google says you can upload up to 100 additional business photos and manage up to 6 callouts per category. Better Business Bureau callouts are no longer supported, so if you had one, replace it with other callouts such as specialties or accepted payment methods. Source: Google Ads Help

To stop leads for a while, Google says to open Settings, select the campaign and switch its status to Paused, then back to Enabled to start getting leads again.

What you still change on the Business Profile

Some details can’t be edited in Google Ads at all. Google says your business name, address and hours “sync one-way to Google Ads and cannot be edited there.” So if your hours change for the holidays, change them on the Google Business Profile. Source: Google Ads Help

  • Small changes sync on their own. Google says changes to hours, phone number and minor name updates sync within 24 hours.
  • Big changes trigger a review. A significant change to the business name, storefront address or primary category needs verification. Google says the review typically takes 24 to 48 hours and the campaign may pause while it happens. Make those changes on a quiet day, not the Monday after a storm.
  • One thing you can now change yourself: the phone number your call and message leads are routed to, in real time, in Google Ads.

The reverse is also true. Google says service areas, ad schedules and business categories operate independently of your Business Profile after migration, so you can change your ad targeting in Google Ads without touching the profile.

Reading the campaign screen

The campaign overview uses Google Ads words. Google spells out the three that matter:

Google Ads labelWhat it means for a Local Services campaign
ConversionsThe number of leads you were charged for
CostYour total ad spend
Cost / conv.Your average cost per lead

So “twelve conversions” means twelve charged leads, not twelve customers. Whether those leads turned into booked jobs is something Google Ads doesn’t know unless someone on your side tracks it. Agencies reporting this to clients can use our guide to LSA reporting for agencies.

Lead emails and last year’s reports

Lead emails. Google says email alerts for call and message leads are now controlled from Notifications, under the Admin menu. Every account user and direct manager is opted in by default, regardless of what you set in the old dashboard. Anyone can turn theirs off, but the account needs at least one user to stay opted in. Source: Google Ads Help Worth a look: if a former employee or an old agency still has access, they’re getting your lead emails too. Google’s lead alerts are emails; our guide to LSA lead notifications covers getting an instant text for every lead.

Old reports. This is the one that hurts. Google says previous campaign performance reports, such as past impressions, clicks and weekly spend, don’t migrate to Google Ads and can’t be opened after migration. Google says you’ll be able to download historical reports “soon” from its redirection page, and that if you need them now, take screenshots before migrating. Who still uses the old dashboard, and who to call

Not everyone is in Google Ads yet. Google says businesses in categories that need advanced verification, such as locksmiths or garage door services, or outside the currently supported regions, are directed to the legacy Local Services portal at g.co/localservices, and those categories come to pay-per-lead goals in Performance Max in 2027. Source: Google Ads Help

New campaigns are also different. Google’s migration FAQ says you can’t create new pay-per-lead campaigns directly in Google Ads “at this time,” and that additional campaigns or local accounts still start at g.co/localservices. Source: Google Ads Help Setting up a brand-new client? That’s its own process, covered step by step in our guide to setting up Local Services Ads for a client.

If something is wrong with a migrated account, Google lists a dedicated support line for migrating Local Services advertisers: 1-844-205-0011, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern. For every other kind of LSA problem and who to contact for it, see our guide to contacting Google Local Services Ads support.

Looking for the app? Google’s migration page doesn’t mention a mobile app for pay-per-lead campaigns. For agencies: the “where are my leads?” call

If you run Local Services Ads for clients, you’re already getting these calls. “Where are my leads?” “Why does it say conversions?” “Where did my weekly budget go?” Send the client this page or the video instead of a support ticket. Three things are worth doing for every migrated client:

  1. Check Admin → Notifications. Make sure the client and the right people on your team get lead emails, and remove anyone who shouldn’t.
  2. Save what reports you can. For clients who haven’t migrated yet, screenshot or download last year’s numbers now, because January year-over-year reporting depends on them.
  3. Re-explain the numbers once. Conversions are charged leads, and the daily budget times 30.4 is the monthly cap. Saying it once on a call saves a lot of confused emails.

Then use the call. The client who can’t find their leads usually can’t tell you how many calls went to voicemail either, or whether anyone replied to last week’s messages. That problem didn’t move to Google Ads. It came with them. If you manage several migrated clients, our agency migration playbook has the full runbook.

Frequently asked questions

How do I log in to Local Services Ads now?

If your account has moved to Google Ads, log in to Google Ads with the same Google login that had access to the old Local Services Ads dashboard. Google says that once an account migrates, trying to log in to the old dashboard automatically redirects you to the campaign overview in Google Ads. If your account hasn’t moved yet, the old dashboard still works until your migration date.

Where are my Local Services Ads leads in Google Ads?

Open the Goals menu, click Conversions, then Leads. Google says this Leads page replaces the Local Services Ads lead inbox, with charged status, lead type, a lead feedback form and a download option. Past lead history, including customer contact details, message history and older call recordings, transfers to the Leads page.

Can I still use the old LSA dashboard?

Only until your account migrates. Google says that after migration you can no longer access the original dashboard. Businesses in categories that need advanced verification, such as locksmiths or garage door services, and businesses outside the supported regions are directed to the legacy Local Services portal at g.co/localservices for now.

What does “Conversions” mean on a Local Services campaign in Google Ads?

Google says Conversions is the number of leads you were charged for. Cost is your total ad spend, and Cost / conv. is your average cost per lead. Twelve conversions means twelve charged leads, not twelve booked jobs.

Do my old Local Services Ads reports carry over to Google Ads?

No. Google says past campaign performance reports, such as impressions, clicks and weekly spend, don’t migrate and can’t be opened after migration. Google says a download page for historical reports will be available soon, and that if you need the reports now you should take screenshots before your account migrates.
