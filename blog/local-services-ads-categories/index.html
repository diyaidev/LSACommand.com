Google Local Services Ads Categories: The 2026 Eligibility List for Agencies

Before you sell Local Services Ads to a client, check two things: is their business type on Google’s list, and is it open where they work? Here’s the current US category list, grouped the way Google groups it, plus the restrictions that decide how long onboarding takes.

Every agency that sells Local Services Ads (LSA) eventually signs a client, starts onboarding, and finds out the business can’t run them. Sometimes the category isn’t on Google’s list. Sometimes it’s on the list but not open in the client’s state. Sometimes it’s open but needs checks that will take a month.

All of that is knowable before you send a proposal. This guide is the pre-sale check: what Google lists, where the limits are, and what each category means for onboarding time. Everything below comes from Google’s Local Services help center unless we say otherwise.

How LSA eligibility works

Google says Local Services Ads are “available only for certain service categories,” and eligibility depends on both the business category and its location. Source: Google Local Services Help

That gives you three separate questions for every prospect:

  1. Is the category on the list? If the client’s main service isn’t a Google category, no amount of setup will fix it.
  2. Is it open in their area? Some categories are only open in certain states or cities. Google’s getting-started page links to a “Check eligibility” tool that confirms whether LSA is available for a business type in your area, and the sign-up flow has the same step at ads.google.com/localservices/signup/eligibility.
  3. What does screening require? Requirements differ by category, and they decide how long it takes to go live.

Google’s help center has country pages for Austria, Belgium, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States. The list below is the US list. Category coverage outside the US is narrower, so run the eligibility check for every non-US client.

Restricted and pilot categories

Some categories are on the list but not open everywhere.

  • California and Florida only: architect, drain expert, home insulation, insurance agency, interior designer, piercing studio and tattoo studio. Source: Google Local Services Help Google’s requirements page also marks waxing or hair removal as California and Florida only.
  • Food and beverage is a pilot. Google says restaurant and coffee and dessert shop categories are “in a pilot phase with restricted geographic availability.” Sterling Sky reported the pilot cities as Atlanta, Boston, Indianapolis, Miami and San Jose. [Ryan confirm before publishing: Google doesn’t publish the pilot city list; this comes from Sterling Sky.]

If a prospect’s category is restricted, don’t promise a launch date until the eligibility check passes for their area.

Which categories get the Google Verified badge

The Google Verified badge is what most clients picture when they ask for “those Google ads with the check mark.” It isn’t available everywhere. Google says the badge is currently unavailable for the auto, beauty and dining categories. Source: Google Local Services Help Our Google Verified badge guide covers what replaced Google Guaranteed and Google Screened.

Google also offers pre-badge ads, which let a business receive leads before it finishes every onboarding step. Pre-badge listings rank below businesses that have completed onboarding. Google lists three exceptions where pre-badge ads aren’t available: garage door services, health care, and locksmiths. For those clients, nothing runs until screening is done.

What each category means for onboarding time

Every category needs a verified Google Business Profile. Beyond that, screening varies by group, and the differences decide whether a client is live in days or weeks. Google applies many checks to “select users” on a risk basis, so two clients in the same category can get different requests. Source: Google Local Services Help

Two category rules that catch agencies out:

  • Field staff count. In urgent categories, Google’s background checks extend to employees, contractors and subcontractors who serve customers directly. A client with ten techs has ten people to clear, not one.
  • Location-level licenses. Google says multi-location businesses need licenses for each location. A three-location HVAC client is three sets of paperwork.

Features differ by category too

Even after a client is live, some LSA features depend on the category and country.

  • Booking leads aren’t available for health care. Health care clients get calls and messages only.
  • Some tools are US and Canada only. Google lists booking tracking, lead credit tracking and review requests as available in the US and Canada only.
  • No lead credits in some categories. Google says lead credits aren’t available for health care, tax specialists, or advertisers in Europe, the Middle East and Africa. Source: Google Local Services Help Price those clients knowing a bad lead stays charged.
  • Health care leads don’t come through the API. Google’s developer documentation says leads in health care categories aren’t available through the Google Ads API. Source: Google Ads API documentation If your reporting runs on the API, a dental or chiropractic client will need a different reporting method. Category also decides when a client migrates

Google is moving LSA into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, and the schedule is by category: select US home and storefront categories from August 2026, broader groups including service-area businesses without storefronts in late 2026, and non-US accounts and remaining categories in 2027. Source: Google Ads Help

Google also says businesses in categories that need advanced verification, such as locksmiths and garage door services, manage campaigns in the legacy LSA portal for now. Source: Google Ads Help So a mixed client book can have some accounts in Google Ads and some still in the old dashboard for months. Our agency migration playbook covers how to run both.

A pre-sale category check for every prospect

Run this before a proposal goes out. It takes about ten minutes per prospect.

  1. Match the main service to a Google category. Use the client’s core revenue service, not their whole service menu. A remodeler who mostly does kitchens fits “kitchen remodeling”; one who mostly builds decks may not fit anything.
  2. Run Google’s eligibility check for the business type and the client’s main service area.
  3. Note the badge and pre-badge status. Auto, beauty and dining clients won’t get the badge. Garage door, locksmith and health care clients won’t get pre-badge leads.
  4. List the screening steps from the table above, and who has to do each one. Owner ID and background checks can’t be done by the agency.
  5. Count the people and locations that need checks or licenses.
  6. Set the launch expectation in writing. A plumber with two techs and a clean license history is a different timeline from a five-location locksmith.

Frequently asked questions

What categories are eligible for Google Local Services Ads?

In the US, Google lists home services, auto care, beauty, health care, legal and other professional services, learning, child care and funeral homes, pet care, wellness, storage and a few others, plus a restaurant pilot. Availability also depends on the business’s location, so confirm with Google’s eligibility check.

Why does it say my business isn’t eligible for Local Services Ads?

Usually because the business category isn’t on Google’s list, or it is but isn’t open in that area yet. Some categories are only open in California and Florida, and restaurant categories are a limited pilot.

Are Local Services Ads available outside the US?

Yes, in some countries. Google’s help center has country pages for Austria, Belgium, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Spain, Switzerland and the United Kingdom as well as the US, but category coverage outside the US is narrower.

Do all LSA categories get the Google Verified badge?

No. Google says the badge is currently unavailable for auto, beauty and dining categories.

Which LSA categories take longest to approve?

Garage door and locksmith clients need Advanced Verification and get no pre-badge ads. Electricians, HVAC and plumbers need background checks on field staff. Health care needs provider checks and an NPI. Plan extra time for all of them.
