Google Local Services Ads Background Check and Verification: The Agency Checklist

Most stalled LSA launches aren’t a Google problem. They’re a missing document, a mismatched name, or a verification email nobody opened. Here’s the checklist we use before a client account goes live.

Agencies selling Local Services Ads tend to promise a launch date before the client has passed Google’s checks. Then the account sits in “pending” for weeks, the client wonders what they’re paying you for, and the first month of the relationship is spent chasing paperwork.

Verification is predictable if you treat it as a project with a checklist and an owner. Everything below comes from Google’s own help center unless we say otherwise. The requirements vary by category and location, so the client’s verification dashboard is always the final word.

What Google checks, and why it varies

Google says every business must pass its screening and verification process to run Local Services Ads, and that the process varies by business category and location. It can include background checks, business registration, insurance and license checks, and minimum review requirements. Source: Google Local Services Help

Since late 2025 the old Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified badges have been folded into a single Google Verified badge. Passing these checks is what earns it. Our Google Verified badge guide covers that change.

Here is what can be asked of a US client, and who actually has to do it:

The pattern to notice: the steps that stall launches are the ones only the owner can do. An agency can prepare almost everything, but it can’t take a client’s background check or show their ID for them.

The Business Profile step

Google matches each LSA account to the business’s Google Business Profile. To pass this step, the person completing the business checks must be a profile owner or manager, even if someone else at the business already verified the profile. If the signed-in user isn’t, the dashboard offers an option to request access. That person can carry on with other steps, but this one must be finished before the ad can appear in search results, and Google says it can take up to 24 hours to show as done.

Agency move: confirm before kickoff who owns the Business Profile, and have the person who will do LSA verification added as an owner or manager first.

Business registration and D-U-N-S

For select verticals in the US, Google checks registration using the business’s D-U-N-S number from Dun & Bradstreet. Many businesses already have one; you can look it up on D&B’s site using the same name and address as the Business Profile. If the client needs a new one, Google notes the application can take up to 30 days. That is the single biggest reason to start verification before you promise a launch date.

If the client can’t get a D-U-N-S number, Google accepts a Secretary of State business registration, an EIN document from the IRS, or a Department of Revenue registration.

Business representative and ID check

In the US, for select verticals, Google confirms that the person setting up the account is authorized to represent the business. First it checks the representative against Dun & Bradstreet’s list of business representatives. If they aren’t listed, a registration document showing both the representative’s full name and the business’s legal name will do. Then Google’s partner Evident runs a private identity check using a clear, unexpired government-issued photo ID.

Google’s own advice is the one that saves the most time: make sure the representative’s full name and the legal business name match exactly across every document.

Background checks

In specific categories and locations, Google requires background checks for the business, the business owner and the field worker roster: employees, contractors, subcontractors and anyone else who performs services at customers’ homes or workplaces. Office staff and customer support who don’t serve customers in person don’t need them.

A few details worth knowing before your client asks:

  • They’re free to the business, and Google says there’s no credit check or credit pull.
  • Every owner must complete one. A business with three partners needs three.
  • Results are confidential between the business and the background check partner. Your agency won’t see them, and Google directs questions about results and appeals to the partner.
  • Multiple locations under the same business name need a business entity check and an owner check per location.
  • If a check fails, the business can contact the partner to correct errors. After a final disqualification, it must wait an extra 30 days to reapply; if a second application is also denied, the wait is 180 days for every reapplication after that.

That last point is why we don’t rush a client with a complicated history into applying. A clean first application is worth a week of preparation.

Insurance

For categories that require it, the business uploads a certificate of insurance, and the minimum amount depends on category and location. The dashboard shows the required amount on the upload screen. Google’s North American upload rules are specific, and most rejections break one of them:

  • The document must be active and valid for at least 14 days after submission.
  • The name must match the name the business advertises under. If it doesn’t, attach a DBA document.
  • The address must cover the service area being advertised.
  • A policy number must be visible.
  • The document must be legitimate and non-editable. Google says a signed certificate of insurance is the best document to provide.

Agency move: get the certificate from the client’s insurance agent directly, check it against this list before uploading, and put the expiry date on your calendar.

Licenses

Google verifies applicable state-level business and owner licenses against state databases where it can, and shows verified licenses on the provider profile. The business must also confirm it holds any county and city licenses and that its service professionals are properly licensed. A business serving several locations has to provide licenses for each one.

Verification doesn’t end at launch

Google now describes a risk-based process for select US advertisers. Some businesses get accelerated verification and the badge quickly, then have 14 days to complete additional verification steps to keep the ad serving. Advertisers are re-evaluated on an ongoing basis, and a change in risk level triggers a follow-up review. Skipping a requested review can cost the badge, pause the ads, or lead to suspension. Google also says businesses may be asked to repeat checks from time to time.

In practice, the most damaging verification failure we see isn’t a rejection. It’s a request for one more step that lands in an inbox nobody is watching, while the account quietly stops running. If a client’s ads vanish, verification is one of the first things to check. See Local Services Ads not showing for the full troubleshooting order.

A related warning for clients: scam robocalls claiming “your business isn’t verified on Google” are common. Real verification requests appear in the LSA or Google Ads dashboard. Tell clients to check there, not to act on a phone call.

The pre-flight checklist we use

Before an agency commits to a launch date, collect and check:

  1. Names. Legal name, DBA, Business Profile name, insurance name, license name and D-U-N-S record should all agree, or you have the DBA paperwork to bridge them.
  2. Business Profile access. The person doing verification is an owner or manager of the profile.
  3. D-U-N-S. Looked up, or applied for, with up to 30 days built into the timeline.
  4. Representative ID. Current government photo ID, with the name matching the registration documents.
  5. Owners and field workers. A list of every owner and every worker who will need a background check, and a plan for getting each of them to complete it.
  6. Insurance certificate. Signed, with a policy number, valid at least 14 days past upload, at or above the category minimum.
  7. Licenses. State license numbers, plus confirmation of local licenses for every location served.
  8. Renewal dates. Insurance and license expiry dates on your team’s calendar.
  9. An inbox someone reads. The address that receives verification mail is monitored by the owner or by your team.

Then set the client’s expectation in writing: launch happens when Google finishes verification, and the steps only the owner can do are on them.

Frequently asked questions

Do Google Local Services Ads require a background check?

In specific categories and locations, yes. Google requires background checks for the business, every owner, and field workers who provide services at customers’ properties. Check the client’s verification dashboard for their exact requirements.

Who runs the Local Services Ads background check, and does it cost anything?

Google’s local background check partners run them, and Google says businesses aren’t charged. The checks don’t involve a credit check. Separately, Google’s partner Evident handles the identity check for the business representative.

Can an agency complete LSA verification for a client?

An agency can prepare documents and manage the process, but the owner, the business representative and each field worker have to complete their own identity and background checks.

What happens if a business fails the LSA background check?

It isn’t eligible for the program it applied to. It can contact the background check partner to correct errors. After a final disqualification it must wait an additional 30 days to reapply, and 180 days after a second denial.

Why was our LSA insurance certificate rejected?

The usual reasons: it expires within 14 days of submission, the name doesn’t match the advertised business name (without a DBA), the address doesn’t cover the service area, there’s no policy number, the coverage is below the category minimum, or the document is editable.
