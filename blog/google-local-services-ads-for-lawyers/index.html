Google Local Services Ads for Lawyers: What Replaced Google Screened, and How to Run It in 2026

Plenty of guides still talk about the Google Screened badge. It’s gone. Here’s what Google checks for law firms now, how firms are charged, and the intake problems that decide whether legal LSA pays.

Legal is one of the few Local Services Ads categories outside home services, and it behaves differently. The cases are worth more, the person answering the phone is an intake specialist rather than the owner, and every lawyer in the firm gets checked against the state bar. The mechanics of LSA are the same. The ways it goes wrong are not.

Facts below come from Google’s help center unless we say otherwise. This isn’t legal advice: every firm should check its state bar’s advertising rules before running any ad.

Google Screened is now Google Verified

For years, law firms needed to be a “Google Screened” business to run Local Services Ads, and the green Google Screened check mark sat on legal ads. Clio’s guide is a good snapshot of that era. Google Guaranteed was the home-services equivalent, backed by Google’s money-back guarantee.

Starting in October 2025, Google consolidated Google Guaranteed, Google Screened, License Verified by Google and the money-back guarantee program into a single Google Verified badge. Search Engine Journal covered the announcement. For a law firm, that means:

  • The checks still exist. The badge is earned by passing Google’s screening for the category and location. Only the name changed.
  • It can sit next to the firm or the lawyer. Google shows the badge next to the business name or next to its featured professionals. Source: Google, Getting started with Local Services Ads
  • It isn’t guaranteed on every impression. Search Engine Journal reported that Google may show the badge only when it predicts it will help people decide. Don’t promise a client the badge on every ad.
  • The old wording has to go. Remove “Google Screened” from the firm’s website, intake scripts and other ads. Our Google Verified badge guide covers the change in more detail.

Which practice areas can run Local Services Ads

In the US, Google’s screening requirements list 17 lawyer categories: bankruptcy, business, contract, criminal, disability, DUI, estate, family, immigration, IP, labor, litigation, malpractice, personal injury, real estate, tax and traffic. Source: Google, Business screening and verification requirements Requirements vary by location, so the account’s verification dashboard is always the final word.

A multi-practice firm picks the categories that match the work it wants. Each one is another set of leads somebody has to answer, so start with the practice areas the intake team can actually handle.

What Google checks for law firms

Google lists the same requirements for each lawyer category, with one exception for bankruptcy:

Google says screening averages 3 to 4 weeks after documents are submitted. Because the bar check runs per lawyer, a firm that adds attorneys adds checks. The document rules that stall home-services launches (names that don’t match, an expiring certificate, nobody with Business Profile access) stall legal launches too. Law firms can also add featured professionals and education details to the account. Source: Google, Edit your ad or business information Someone choosing a lawyer cares who they’ll actually talk to, so fill these in for the attorneys who take the cases the firm wants.

What “Google Screened” costs, and what firms actually pay for

“Google Screened cost” is a common search. Google’s help center doesn’t list a fee for the badge itself. What a firm pays for is leads, on the same rules as every other LSA advertiser: Source: Google, How leads work

  • Phone leads are charged when the firm answers or calls back and speaks with the person.
  • Message leads are charged when the firm receives a text or email from the person.
  • Booking leads are charged when a booking request comes in (US and Canada only).
  • A voicemail from a prospective client counts as a valid lead.
  • Missed calls during business hours can be charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. Reaching voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours counts as connected.
  • Repeat contacts from the same number or email aren’t charged again if there’s been interaction within 15 days.

Google doesn’t publish lead prices for legal categories, and the figures you’ll see quoted online vary widely by practice area and city. Use the account’s own data, and size the budget with the method in our LSA budget and bidding guide: leads needed per week, times expected cost per lead.

On credits: Google reassesses charged leads over 30 days and automatically credits ones it finds poor quality, duplicate or invalid. It excludes health care categories and “tax specialists” from lead credits. Tax specialists are a separate category from tax lawyers, so check which one a client is in.

Intake decides whether legal LSA pays

In home services, the person who answers is often the owner or a dispatcher. In a law firm it’s a receptionist, an intake specialist or a voicemail box. That’s where legal LSA budgets leak.

  • Business hours are billing hours. Missed calls are billable during the hours on the firm’s Google Business Profile. A firm that lists 8 to 6 but has one receptionist who takes lunch at noon is paying for calls nobody picks up. Either cover the hours or list them honestly.
  • Voicemail isn’t protection. A business-hours call that reaches voicemail counts as connected and can be charged after 20 seconds, message or no message.
  • Responsiveness feeds ranking. Average response time is part of profile quality, and Google says missed calls may hurt responsiveness. Source: Google, How ad ranking works
  • Message leads need an owner. If intake doesn’t work the message inbox, leave message leads off until someone does. A slow reply hurts the whole account.
  • Rate every lead. The lead feedback survey is how Google learns which inquiries the firm wants. Google no longer credits leads for job types or areas the advertiser doesn’t serve, so a practice area that keeps sending the wrong cases has to be fixed in the settings, not disputed. Source: Google, lead credits

Before any spend, ask three questions: who answers at 12:30 p.m., at 5:45 p.m. and on Saturday morning; what happens to a voicemail; and who replies to a message lead, and how fast. The answers predict the results better than the budget does.

How legal LSA ads rank

Legal ads rank on the same inputs as every other category: the bid; the likelihood of a lead (responsiveness, relevance, enabled lead types and search context); and profile quality (rating, number of reviews, average response time and completed verification checks). There are no keywords to buy. For a firm with several offices, Google shows only the highest-ranking ad for a query, so offices don’t stack on one search.

Reviews matter early. Google recommends 5 or more, and some business types need 5 before the ad will show. Source: Google, Improve your Local Services Ads performance Our guide on ranking higher in LSA covers the rest.

The move into Google Ads

Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as a Performance Max campaign with pay-per-lead goals. Its transition page names home and storefront services in the first wave (August 2026), service-area businesses and accounts with custom bidding or booking in late 2026, and non-US accounts and remaining categories in 2027. It doesn’t name legal categories specifically, so watch for the email the account administrator receives 14 days before an account moves. Source: Google Ads Help, LSA transition

When a firm’s account moves:

  • Manual max-per-lead bids end, and one campaign-level target applies across the campaign’s categories. A firm that wants different bidding for, say, personal injury and estate planning should consider separate campaigns for each.
  • Past performance reports don’t carry over. Past leads, message history and call recordings do. Take screenshots of anything you’ll need.
  • Verification carries over, and Google says insurance and license reverification are no longer required after the move.

Checklist for agencies running LSA for law firms

  1. Confirm the categories match the practice areas the firm wants, and that intake can handle each one.
  2. Collect every attorney’s bar details, the insurance certificate and Business Profile access before you promise a launch date. Build in 3 to 4 weeks.
  3. Replace “Google Screened” wording across the firm’s website, scripts and ads.
  4. Fill in featured professionals and education details.
  5. Set business hours to match real phone coverage.
  6. Decide who answers calls, voicemails and messages, and agree on a response time.
  7. Rate every lead, and check charge statuses weekly.
  8. Track consultations booked and cases signed per lead, not just lead count.
  9. Link the account to your manager account and document settings before the Google Ads move.
  10. Have the firm confirm its state bar’s advertising rules.

Frequently asked questions

Can lawyers use Google Local Services Ads?

Yes. In the US, Google lists 17 lawyer categories, including personal injury, family, criminal, immigration, estate and bankruptcy. Firms must pass Google’s screening, which includes a state bar license check for each lawyer in the firm.

Does Google Screened still exist?

No. Starting in October 2025, Google consolidated Google Screened, Google Guaranteed and License Verified by Google into one Google Verified badge. The screening checks still apply; only the badge changed.

How much does Google Screened cost?

Google’s help center doesn’t list a fee for the badge itself. Firms pay per valid lead: answered calls, messages, booking requests and voicemails. Missed calls during business hours can be charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds.

What’s the difference between Google Screened and Google Guaranteed?

Google Screened was the badge for professional services such as law firms. Google Guaranteed was the home-services badge backed by Google’s money-back guarantee. Both are now the Google Verified badge, and the guarantee program has ended.

How long does Local Services Ads verification take for a law firm?

Google says screening averages 3 to 4 weeks after documents are submitted. Bar checks run for each lawyer, and mismatched names or missing documents are the usual causes of delay.
