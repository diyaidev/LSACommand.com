Local Services Ads vs. Google Search Ads: Which Should Contractors Use?

Both put you at the top of Google, but they work very differently. Here’s how Local Services Ads and Google Search Ads compare for home service businesses.

The short version

Landing pageYour LSA profileYour website
Ranking signalsProximity, reviews, responsiveness, hours, budgetBid, ad and landing-page quality

When LSA wins

LSA usually wins for urgent, local, phone-first jobs such as a broken AC, a burst pipe, or a roof leak. The homeowner sees reviews and the Google Verified badge, then calls directly. You only pay when a lead comes in, and invalid leads can be credited.

The catch: you have less control. You can’t pick keywords or write ad copy, and ranking depends heavily on how fast you answer. Businesses that miss calls slide down. See how LSA ranking works.

When Search Ads win

Search Ads give you control: specific keywords, custom landing pages, and your own messaging. They’re useful for high-ticket planned projects, services LSA doesn’t cover, and capturing searches outside your LSA categories. You pay for every click, though, whether or not it turns into a lead.

Most contractors should run both, and answer everything

LSA and Search Ads cover different parts of the same search results page. Running both lets you appear twice. Either way, the money is made or lost when the lead arrives. Frequently asked questions

Are Local Services Ads cheaper than Google Ads?

It depends on your trade and market. LSA charges per lead instead of per click, which often makes lead cost more predictable, but the real comparison is cost per booked job.

Can I run Local Services Ads and Google Ads at the same time?

Yes. They appear in different places on the results page, and many contractors run both. LSA is also moving into Google Ads as a Performance Max campaign type.

Do Local Services Ads use keywords?

No. LSA matches you to searches based on your business category, job types, and service area.
