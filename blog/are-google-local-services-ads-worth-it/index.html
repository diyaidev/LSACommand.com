Every home-service owner asks it before they sign: are Local Services Ads worth the money? The honest answer is “it depends on three numbers,” and an agency that can show those numbers wins the account. Here’s the math, the cases where LSA isn’t worth it, and how to walk a client through it.

“Are Local Services Ads worth it?” is the question behind most LSA sales calls. Clients have usually been burned by ads before, and they want to know if this one is different.

The honest answer is that it depends on three numbers: what a lead costs, how many leads turn into booked jobs, and what a job is worth. An agency that can put those three on one page, for that client, is the agency that gets the account. This guide covers how billing works, what leads cost in 2026, the math, and the cases where we’d tell a client not to bother.

How you pay for Local Services Ads

LSA isn’t pay per click. Google says you’re “charged for each valid lead you receive,” where a valid lead is one related to your business. Source: Google Ads Help In practice that means:

  • Answered calls, messages, voicemails and bookings can be charged. So can a missed call during business hours if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds.
  • Repeat contact is free for 15 days. Once a lead is charged, Google doesn’t charge follow-up calls or messages from the same number or email if there’s interaction within 15 days.
  • Junk is filtered and credited. Google says leads judged invalid or low quality at first contact aren’t charged, and charged leads are reassessed over 30 days and credited automatically if they turn out poor quality, duplicate or invalid. Google says lead credits aren’t available for health care verticals, tax specialists or advertisers in EMEA.
  • Spend is capped. Once the monthly max is reached, the ad stops showing for the rest of the month unless the budget changes.

That billing model is the strongest part of the pitch. The client pays when a customer contacts them, not when someone browses. The catch is just as important: a lead you don’t answer is still a lead you paid for.

The math that answers “is it worth it”

Cost per lead is the wrong number to judge LSA on. The number that matters is cost per booked job:

Cost per booked job = cost per lead ÷ book rate

Using SearchLight’s blended averages, $53 ÷ 43.9% comes to about $121 per booked job, against an average ticket of $1,826. That’s what makes LSA attractive for most home-service trades: even a lot of unbooked leads can be covered by one job.

To run it for a prospect, you need four inputs: their average job value, their rough gross margin, a realistic cost per lead for their trade and area, and an honest estimate of how many calls they actually answer.

When LSA isn’t worth it

Saying no to the wrong client protects your agency’s reputation. We’d hold off when:

  • The category isn’t eligible in their area. Check first with our categories guide.
  • Nobody answers the phone during ad hours. Missed business-hours calls can be charged, and Google says missed calls can hurt responsiveness, which affects ranking. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • The job value is too low. If a booked job is worth less than its booked-job cost plus overhead, more leads lose money faster.
  • The business is already at capacity. Leads it can’t take still cost money. Pause instead (see how to pause Local Services Ads).
  • The profile can’t compete yet. Google says rating and number of reviews affect LSA ranking, and it suggests aiming for 5 or more Business Profile reviews with ratings above four stars. Source: Google Ads Help A two-review profile next to competitors with hundreds will struggle.
  • They can’t pass verification. If the category needs a license or insurance the business doesn’t have, the account won’t go live.

LSA or Google Search ads?

Clients often ask this next. The short version: LSA charges per lead, appears in its own unit on Search and Maps, and needs no keywords or landing page. Search ads charge per click and give you full control over keywords, ad copy and landing pages. Google itself recommends running pay-per-lead campaigns alongside Search and other campaign types. Source: Google Ads Help Our Local Services Ads vs. Google Search Ads comparison goes deeper.

How agencies make the case to a client

Here’s the sequence we’d use on a sales call with a home-service owner:

  1. Show them the LSA unit. Search their main service in their town and point at who’s already there, with their ratings and review counts. It’s more convincing than any slide.
  2. Check eligibility and verification live. Confirm the category and area qualify, and list what their verification will need.
  3. Run the math with their numbers. Use the four inputs above. Let them supply the job value and margin; you supply a cost-per-lead range for their trade with its source.
  4. Name the risk honestly. Unanswered leads still cost money. Then show how you’ll handle that: who answers, how fast messages get replies, and how leads get rated.
  5. Promise the right report. Tell them you’ll report on booked jobs and cost per booked job, not just lead count. Our LSA client report template is built around it.

Frequently asked questions

Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for small businesses?

Often, yes, when the business is in an eligible category, can answer calls during ad hours, and has an average job value well above the cost of a booked job. You pay per valid lead rather than per click, so the risk is lower than most ad types, but unanswered leads still cost money.

How much does a Local Services Ads lead cost?

Google doesn’t publish an average and says prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding. SearchLight Digital’s February 2026 benchmark of 888 home-service contractors put the blended cost per lead at $53, with electrical at $39, HVAC at $51 and plumbing at $57.

Do you pay for bad leads on Local Services Ads?

Google says it doesn’t charge leads it judges invalid or low quality at first contact, reassesses charged leads over 30 days and credits poor-quality, duplicate or invalid ones automatically. A real lead that’s a poor fit can still be charged, so rate it through the lead feedback survey to help Google send fewer like it.

Is LSA better than Google Search ads?

They do different jobs. LSA charges per lead and shows at the top for service searches with the Google Verified badge where available. Search ads charge per click and give more control over keywords and landing pages. Many home-service businesses run both.

When are Local Services Ads not worth it?

When the category isn’t eligible, when nobody answers the phone during ad hours, when the job value is too low to cover a booked-job cost, when the business can’t take more work, or when the profile has too few reviews to compete.
