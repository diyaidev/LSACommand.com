LSA vs. Google Ads: What Agencies Should Recommend to Each Client

“Should we run LSA or Google Ads?” is a question every home-service client asks. Here’s a framework agencies can use to answer it for each client, what the move to Performance Max changes, and how to present the recommendation.

Five fit checks for each client

Run every client through these before you recommend anything.

1. Is the client eligible and verifiable?

LSA only works in supported categories, and the business has to pass Google’s screening. If verification is going to be a struggle (licensing gaps, owners who won’t do background checks), lead with Search and start LSA verification alongside it. Our verification checklist covers the steps.

2. Can the client answer the phone?

This is the question agencies skip. On LSA, a missed call during business hours where the caller stays on more than 20 seconds is a charged lead. Source: Google Local Services Help Missed calls can also hurt responsiveness, which Google uses in ranking. Source: Google Local Services Help A client who lets calls ring out will pay for leads it never talks to and slide down the rankings. Fix lead handling first, or the LSA recommendation will fail.

3. Does the client have reviews?

Rating and number of reviews feed LSA profile quality. Source: Google Local Services Help A new business with few reviews may get more from Search early on, while a review program builds up.

4. What jobs does the client actually want?

LSA targets by job type and area, not keywords. If the client only wants specific, high-ticket jobs, Search keywords and landing pages give you more precision. If the client takes most jobs in its category, LSA’s broad, call-ready demand fits well.

5. How much does the client need to control?

LSA gives you few levers. Search gives you many, but needs ad copy, landing pages and ongoing management. Match the channel to the work the client is paying you to do.

A simple recommendation matrix

This is a starting point, not a rule. Most established home-service clients end up running both, because they catch different searches.

How to sequence the budget

When a client has one budget, the order matters more than the split. A practical sequence:

  1. Lead handling. Make sure calls get answered and messages get a reply. Without that, any spend leaks.
  2. LSA at a budget the client can sustain. LSA budgets are set as a weekly average with a monthly cap. Source: Google Local Services Help After the move to Performance Max, the budget becomes a daily average, with monthly spend capped at 30.4 times that. Source: Google Ads Help See our budget and bidding guide.
  3. Search for the gaps. Add Search for job types and areas LSA doesn’t cover well, using the client’s own lead data to choose keywords.

We don’t publish typical cost-per-lead numbers here, because they vary widely by trade and market. Use each client’s own account data. Our LSA cost guide explains what drives the price.

What Performance Max changes for this decision

Google is moving LSA into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. Here’s what that does and doesn’t change for your recommendation:

  • The pricing model stays. Google says advertisers still pay only for valid leads rather than clicks. Standard Performance Max campaigns, by contrast, charge per click. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Placements stay. Pay-per-lead campaigns show only on Google Search and Maps, in the same ad positions as LSA. Standard Performance Max serves across Google’s networks, including YouTube and Gmail. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Management moves into Google Ads. Budgets, targeting and the Leads page now live in the same platform as the client’s Search campaigns, which makes running both simpler. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Bidding changes. Manual bidding and vertical-level target CPA are being deprecated. Source: Google Ads Help

So “LSA vs. Google Ads” is now really “pay-per-lead campaign vs. pay-per-click campaign”, inside the same account. The fit checks above still decide it. For more, see our guide to managing LSA for multiple clients after Performance Max.

How to present the recommendation

Clients compare channels by cost, so frame it around what they pay for:

  • LSA: “You pay when a customer calls or messages, so answering every lead is part of the plan.”
  • Search: “You pay for clicks, so the landing page and the follow-up decide what each click is worth.”
  • Both: “LSA catches people who want to call a verified local pro now. Search catches people looking for specific jobs.”

Then report both on the same basis: leads, booked jobs and cost per booked job. Our LSA client report template is built for that.

Frequently asked questions

Should my agency recommend Local Services Ads or Google Ads?

It depends on the client. LSA fits eligible, verified businesses that answer calls and have reviews, because clients pay per valid lead. Search fits clients that need precise keyword control or aren’t eligible yet. Most established home-service clients end up running both.

Is LSA cheaper than Google Search ads?

Not necessarily. LSA charges per valid lead and Search charges per click, so they aren’t directly comparable. Compare cost per booked job using each client’s own data, and remember missed calls on LSA can still be charged.

Does LSA moving to Performance Max mean it’s just Google Ads now?

LSA is moving into the Google Ads platform as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. Google says those campaigns keep the pay-per-lead model and only show on Google Search and Maps, unlike standard Performance Max, which charges per click and serves across Google’s networks.

Can a client run LSA and Search ads at the same time?

Yes. They show in different placements and catch different searches. After the move to Performance Max, both are managed in the same Google Ads platform, which makes running and reporting on both easier.

What should an agency fix before recommending LSA?

Lead handling. On LSA, a missed call during business hours where the caller stays on more than 20 seconds is a charged lead, and missed calls can hurt responsiveness, which Google uses in ranking.
