Local Services Ads Not Showing? Work Through This Checklist

When a client says their Google ads disappeared, the cause is almost always one of nine things. Check them in this order and you’ll usually find it before anyone touches a bid.

Every agency that runs Local Services Ads gets this call: “We stopped getting leads. Are our ads even on?” The fastest way to lose that client is to start changing bids before you know what actually happened.

“Not showing” covers very different problems. An account can be switched off (suspended, paused, unverified, out of budget, billing failure) or it can be on but losing (ranking below competitors, outside the searcher’s area, or not eligible for the job type someone searched). The first group is a status problem with a specific fix. The second is a performance problem. Mixing them up wastes days.

Here is the order we work through it.

Step 1: Separate “off” from “outranked”

Before anything else, open the account’s reports and look at impressions for the last few weeks.

  • Impressions dropped to zero: treat it as a status problem. Something is switching the ad off. Work through steps 2 to 7.
  • Impressions fell but didn’t stop: the ad is running and losing auctions, or it’s showing in fewer places. Skip to steps 8 and 9.

Don’t diagnose by searching on your own phone or the owner’s. LSA results depend on where the searcher is and what exactly they typed, and a handful of manual searches from an office tell you very little. The account’s own reports are the better evidence.

Step 2: Check for a suspension

Google sends both an in-account notification and an email when an LSA account is suspended, and its Policy Manager shows the current status and next steps. Google’s help center is blunt about what a suspension means: a suspended account can’t run any ads or create new content, related LSA accounts may also be suspended, and new accounts the advertiser tries to create may be suspended too. Source: Google Local Services Help

That last point matters for agencies. Never “fix” a suspension by opening a fresh account for the client. It can drag the new account down with the old one and makes the appeal harder.

What actually works:

  1. Read the exact policy cited in the notice. Suspensions point to Google’s Local Services policies, the Local Services terms for providers, or the wider Google Ads policies.
  2. Fix the underlying issue everywhere it shows up: the website, the Business Profile, the documents on file and the business name.
  3. Submit the appeal or support request with documents that match each other exactly: same legal name, same address, same owner.
  4. Keep a written log of what you sent and when, so the next person on your team doesn’t start over.

Step 3: Check verification, including re-verification

Verification isn’t a one-time hurdle any more. Google describes a risk-based process for select US advertisers: some businesses get accelerated verification and the badge quickly, then have 14 days to complete additional verification steps to keep their ad serving. Google also re-evaluates advertisers on an ongoing basis, and if an assessment changes it asks for a follow-up review. Failing to complete a requested review can cost the badge, pause the ads, or lead to suspension. Source: Google Local Services Help

Two verification steps quietly block ads more often than people expect:

  • The Business Profile link. The person completing the business checks must be an owner or manager of the matching Google Business Profile, and this step must be done before an ad can appear in search results. Google says it can take up to 24 hours for the step to show as done.
  • Expiring documents. An insurance certificate or license that lapses can pull a client out of eligibility without anyone touching the campaign.

We cover every check, and who has to do it, in our guide to LSA background checks and verification.

Step 4: Check the switch, the schedule and the hours

The simple causes are the most common, and they are easy to skip because they feel too obvious. Google’s own troubleshooting list for an inactive account starts here: Source: Google Local Services Help

  • Is the ad toggled On? Someone at the client may have paused it during a slow week, a vacation, or a staffing gap.
  • Ad schedule. Under Profile & budget, check the days and times the ad is set to run.
  • Business hours and status. If the business is listed as closed, or as not accepting new customers, it won’t show the way you expect.
  • Change history. In Google Ads, the Change history view shows who changed status or budget and when. That settles “we didn’t touch anything” conversations quickly.

Step 5: Check billing

Unresolved account or billing problems stop ads from running. In Google Ads, the bell icon lists alerts, including ones that say the ad is down, along with the steps to clear them. Expired cards and payment profiles nobody at the client can log into are the usual culprits.

This is also why it matters who controls the payment profile. If the agency can’t see billing alerts, the first sign of a declined card is the client asking where their leads went. Our guide to managing LSA from a manager account covers access levels and billing.

Step 6: Check the budget

If the ad ran at the start of the month and then went quiet, look at the budget. Google’s help center explains that the monthly limit is the average daily budget multiplied by 30.4, and that ads stop once the budget is used up. The “Budget spent” card under Reports shows where you are. Google’s suggested fixes are to raise the budget or switch to the Maximize Leads bid strategy, which it also suggests when impressions are low without a budget problem.

As LSA moves into Google Ads, the old weekly budget is being converted to a daily one, so recheck budgets on any account that has just migrated. More on that in our Performance Max migration guide.

Step 7: Check whether the account has moved into Google Ads

Google is migrating LSA into Google Ads in phases that started in August 2026, and each account moves on its own notified date. Industry coverage reports that once an account migrates, the old LSA dashboard is no longer reachable and logging in redirects to Google Ads, and that historical reporting doesn’t carry over. Source: PPC Land

If a client says “the dashboard looks different and the ads are gone,” check the campaign status in Google Ads before assuming anything is broken. Also check for other Performance Max or Search campaigns in the same account. Google notes that those need their own troubleshooting.

Step 8: Check service area and job types

If impressions dropped but didn’t stop, the ad may simply be eligible in fewer places or for fewer searches than the client thinks.

  • Service area. LSA only shows to people searching in the areas the business targets. A trimmed list of cities or zip codes, or a profile edit that reset the area, narrows reach fast. Our service area targeting page shows how to build the footprint deliberately.
  • Job types. If “water heater installation” is switched off, the business won’t show for it, no matter how the rest of the account looks. Check the job types against the work the client actually wants.
  • Category mismatch. Searches for a service outside the client’s category won’t trigger their ad. Make sure the owner is testing a search the account is eligible for.

Step 9: Check rank

If the account is active, verified, funded and correctly targeted, it’s probably showing, just not near the top. LSA shows a short list of providers, so dropping a few positions can feel like disappearing.

Google ranks LSA advertisers on factors including proximity to the searcher, reviews, responsiveness, business hours and complaints. The one most within reach is responsiveness: missed calls and slow replies to message leads hurt it. Our guide on how to rank higher in Local Services Ads breaks each factor down, and AI call answering closes the missed-call gap that drags many accounts down.

Make it a routine, not a fire drill

If you manage more than a handful of LSA accounts, someone will be in this situation every week. A few habits keep it from turning into a scramble:

  • Watch impressions weekly for every account. A drop to zero should reach your team before it reaches the client.
  • Route verification and policy emails somewhere people actually read. A verification request that sits unread in an owner’s inbox can block a whole account. Our lead notifications guide explains why email-only alerts are risky.
  • Track document expiry dates (insurance, licenses) per client, with reminders well ahead of time.
  • Keep a change log of who changed budget, hours, service area and job types.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Local Services Ad not showing?

The most common causes are a suspension, incomplete verification or a re-verification request, the ad being switched off or scheduled off, billing problems, a used-up budget, and a narrow service area or job type list. If the ad is still getting some impressions, it is usually showing but ranking lower.

Will opening a new account fix a suspended LSA account?

No. Google says new Local Services accounts that a suspended advertiser tries to create may also be suspended. Fix the policy issue and appeal with consistent documents instead.

How long does it take for Local Services Ads to show after verification?

It depends on the account. Google says the Business Profile link step can take up to 24 hours to show as complete, and the ad still has to be switched on, funded and within schedule before it serves.

Does budget affect whether my Local Services Ads show?

Yes. Ads stop once the monthly budget is used up. Google calculates the monthly limit as the average daily budget multiplied by 30.4.

How do I check whether my Local Services Ads are actually running?

Start with impressions in the account’s reports rather than searching on your own phone. Zero impressions points to a status problem; a drop points to ranking, service area or budget.
