Local Services Ads for Multi-Location Businesses: An Agency Guide

Multi-location clients are some of the best LSA accounts an agency can win, and some of the easiest to get wrong. Here’s what Google actually documents about multiple locations, where the risks are, and how agencies set them up.

A client with several locations looks like an easy win for an agency. More locations means more leads and more budget. With Local Services Ads (LSA), though, multi-location setups are where agencies run into verification problems, overlapping ads and policy risk.

Google doesn’t publish one complete “multi-location LSA” rulebook. This guide sticks to what Google does document as of November 2, 2026, and is clear about where you need to confirm with Google before you build.

What Google actually says about multiple locations

  • Overlapping locations don’t multiply exposure. Google’s ranking help says that if a business has multiple locations serving the same geographic area, Local Services will show only the highest ranking ad for a search. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Duplicate accounts to grab traffic are prohibited. Google’s LSA requirements list creating multiple or duplicate accounts to get more traffic, or to get around policy actions, as a violation. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Business details come from the Business Profile. For accounts created from a Google Business Profile, the business name, address and location type are pulled from that profile and can only be edited there. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • The same sync applies after Performance Max. Google says the business name, address and hours sync one way from the Business Profile to Google Ads, and a significant change to the name or storefront address triggers a verification review that can pause the campaign for 24 to 48 hours. Source: Google Ads Help

Put together: every location you advertise should be a real, distinct location with its own verified Business Profile. Adding accounts for the same area doesn’t buy more visibility, and it can look like the duplicate-account behavior Google prohibits.

The overlap problem, and how to plan around it

Because Google shows only the top-ranked ad when locations overlap, two locations bidding on the same towns compete with each other, not with the competition. Before setting up, map each location’s service area:

  • Split territories cleanly. Assign each city or ZIP code to one location, usually the one that actually sends the truck. LSA lets you set service areas by area, and Google lists exceptions for some verticals. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Match job types to each location. If only one location does a certain service, only that location should list that job type.
  • Give each location its own phone line. Leads should reach the people who will do the job. Google’s requirements say leads must be fulfilled by the business or its vetted technicians, and can’t be passed to other businesses. Source: Google Local Services Help

Franchises are a different case

A franchise network is usually several independent businesses under one brand, not one business with several branches. That matters for LSA:

  • Each franchisee is normally its own advertiser, with its own licenses, insurance and verification.
  • Leads can’t be routed from one franchisee to another. Google’s rules prohibit selling or passing leads to other businesses, and say communication with customers should use the same name or branding shown in the ad. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Google’s third-party policy covers agencies managing many advertisers. Batch the work through a manager account and keep each advertiser’s account separate. Source: Google Ads policy

Confirm with Google support how the specific franchise’s structure should be set up before you build. Ownership and licensing details vary, and Google decides what counts as a separate business.

Verification across locations

Verification is per business, and each verified business needs to pass Google’s checks. Two practical points for agencies:

  • Every field worker has to be covered. In categories and locations that require background checks, Google checks the business, the owner and the field worker roster: employees, contractors and subcontractors who provide services at customers’ properties. Source: Google Local Services Help Plan for the checks at every location, not just headquarters. Our verification checklist covers what Google asks for.
  • Don’t move addresses around. Because a significant address change triggers a re-review, avoid editing a location’s Business Profile address to “borrow” another location’s verification. Source: Google Ads Help

Multi-location after the move to Performance Max

Google is migrating LSA into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, in phases from August 2026 through 2027. Source: Google Ads Help For multi-location clients:

  • Locations may migrate on different dates. Plan reporting that works across both the old dashboard and Google Ads during the changeover.
  • New campaigns still start in Local Services. Google says you can’t create new pay-per-lead campaigns in Google Ads yet, and to keep using g.co/localservices to set up additional campaigns or local accounts. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Separate campaigns keep separate bids. Vertical-level target CPA is going away. Google suggests separate campaigns per vertical if you need different bidding rules. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Save each location’s reports. Historical LSA performance reports don’t carry over to Google Ads. Source: Google Ads Help

A setup checklist for multi-location clients

  1. List every location with its address, Business Profile and whether customers visit it.
  2. Confirm each location is a real, distinct business location with a verified Business Profile.
  3. Map service areas so no two locations target the same towns.
  4. Assign job types per location.
  5. Give each location its own lead phone line and its own people to answer it.
  6. Plan verification and background checks for every location’s customer-facing staff.
  7. Connect all accounts to your manager account. See our manager account guide.
  8. Decide who pays for each location. See our LSA billing guide.
  9. Report each location separately, then roll up for the owner.

Frequently asked questions

Can one business run Local Services Ads for multiple locations?

Google documents that when a business has multiple locations serving the same area, only the highest ranking ad shows for a search. Each advertised location should be a real, distinct location with a verified Business Profile. Confirm your client’s specific setup with Google support before building.

Will more LSA accounts for the same area get a client more leads?

No. Google shows only the top-ranked ad when a business’s locations overlap, and its LSA requirements prohibit creating multiple or duplicate accounts to get more traffic.

How should franchises run Local Services Ads?

Each franchisee is usually its own advertiser, with its own verification and account. Leads can’t be passed between businesses under Google’s rules. An agency can manage all of them from one manager account.

Where do business details for each LSA location come from?

For accounts created from a Google Business Profile, the business name, address and location type come from that profile and are edited there. After the move to Performance Max, name, address and hours sync one way from the Business Profile to Google Ads.

Can I create new LSA campaigns for new locations in Google Ads?

Not yet, according to Google. It says to keep using g.co/localservices to set up additional campaigns or local accounts while pay-per-lead campaign creation comes to Google Ads later.
