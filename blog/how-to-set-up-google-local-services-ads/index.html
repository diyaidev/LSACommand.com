How to Set Up Google Local Services Ads for a Client: The 2026 Agency Onboarding Guide

Setting up Local Services Ads for a client used to be one dashboard and a weekly budget. In 2026 it touches the client’s Google Business Profile, Google’s verification checks, the Google Ads migration and your own lead handling. Here’s the order that keeps a new client from stalling halfway.

New Local Services Ads (LSA) clients rarely fail because of the ad. They stall at a verification step nobody was watching, a Business Profile edit that paused the campaign, or a phone that rings out on day one. This guide puts the setup in the order we use for a new client so none of that happens.

It reflects Google’s help pages as of November 1, 2026. Google is moving LSA into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, so a few steps work differently than they did a year ago, and we’ll flag each one.

Step 1: Confirm the client is eligible

Check this before you sell the service, not after. Google says LSA is “available only for certain service categories,” and availability depends on location. Source: Google Local Services Help

  • Category. The client’s main service has to be on Google’s list. Some categories are limited to certain states, such as architects or drain experts, which are listed as California and Florida only. Our Local Services Ads categories guide has the full picture.
  • Location. Run Google’s eligibility check at g.co/localservices with the client’s real address and service area.
  • The Performance Max version is US-only for now. Google’s eligibility rules for pay-per-lead campaigns say the business “must be located and operate in the United States,” and categories that need advanced verification, such as locksmiths and garage door services, stay on the legacy LSA portal during the migration. Source: Google Ads Help

If the category isn’t eligible, stop here and tell the client. It’s a much better conversation now than three weeks into onboarding.

Step 2: Decide who owns the account

Agencies do this both ways. Some open the account in the client’s name and work through a manager account. Others own the account and include it in the service. There’s no Google rule that settles it for you, but the account is tied to the client’s Business Profile and their verification, and the lead history lives inside it.

Whatever you choose, write it into the agreement: who owns the account, who gets admin access, and what happens to access and lead history if the client leaves. Our manager account guide covers linking and access levels in detail.

Step 3: Fix the Google Business Profile first

In 2026 the Business Profile isn’t a side task. Google says you must have at least one verified Business Profile to qualify for pay-per-lead campaigns, and the campaign pulls core details from it. Source: Google Ads Help

  • Name, address and hours sync one way from the Business Profile into Google Ads and can’t be edited in Google Ads. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Big edits trigger a review. Google says significant changes to the business name, storefront address or primary category require verification, and the campaign “may temporarily pause” while that’s reviewed, which typically takes 24 to 48 hours.
  • Hours matter for billing too. Google uses the hours on the Business Profile as “business hours” when it decides whether a missed call is charged. Source: Google Ads Help

So clean up the profile before launch: the correct legal or trading name, the right primary category, real hours, and good photos. Then leave the core fields alone once the ads are live.

Step 4: Sign up at g.co/localservices

This is the step most people expect to be inside Google Ads, and it isn’t yet. Google’s own FAQ says: “You can’t create new campaigns with pay-per-lead goals directly within Google Ads at this time.” New accounts and extra campaigns still start at g.co/localservices, and Google says campaign creation in Google Ads “will become available later.” Source: Google Ads Help

If you’d rather have Google walk you through it, its onboarding line for US advertisers is 1-877-824-3983. Source: Google Local Services Help

Accounts set up this way will be moved into Google Ads later in Google’s rollout. Google is migrating in phases through late 2026 and into 2027, and old LSA performance reports don’t carry over. Save the client’s reports from the start. More in our agency Performance Max migration playbook.

Step 5: Build the profile and targeting

These settings decide which searches trigger the ad, so they’re worth an hour with the client. Google lists them as locations, business categories, service types and ad schedule. Source: Google Ads Help

  • Business categories. Pick only what the client wants more of. Pay-per-lead campaigns take between 1 and 10 categories.
  • Service types. Turn off jobs the client doesn’t want. It’s cheaper than paying for those leads and arguing about credits later.
  • Service area. Google says not to set boundaries “more than two hours of driving time” from the business, and all areas must be in the same country. Inside that limit, it suggests going broad, a whole county rather than single postal codes. Source: Google Ads Help See our service area targeting guide.
  • Ad schedule. Run ads only when someone (or something) will answer. More on why in step 8.
  • Photos and callouts. Google allows up to 100 extra business photos and up to 6 structured callouts per category, such as free estimates or locally owned. BBB callouts are no longer supported. Source: Google Ads Help

Step 6: Get through screening and verification

This is where onboarding timelines slip. Google says the process “varies by business category and location and may include background, business registration, insurance, and license checks, and minimum review requirements.” Source: Google Local Services Help

Three things agencies should know:

  • Risk-based verification. Some US advertisers qualify for accelerated verification and get the Google Verified badge quickly, then have 14 days to finish any extra steps to keep the ad serving. Google re-checks businesses over time, and missing a requested review can mean losing the badge, a paused ad or a suspended account.
  • Background checks are run by Google’s partners, can cover the owner and field workers in some categories, and are free to the business. The results stay between the business and the check partner, so you won’t see them. Your client has to respond to those emails.
  • Pre-badge ads can start early. In many categories, Google lets a business receive leads after preliminary checks while it finishes the rest. Those listings show below fully verified providers, and pre-badge ads aren’t available for garage door services, health care or locksmiths. Source: Google Local Services Help

Give one person on your team ownership of every verification email for every client. For the document-by-document checklist, see our LSA background check and verification guide, and for what the badge means on the ad, the Google Verified badge.

Step 7: Set billing, budget and bidding

Google lists completing billing setup and setting a budget among the steps to go live. Source: Google Local Services Help LSA billing runs through Google Ads, and Google says any suspension of the Google Ads account also suspends LSA. Source: Google Ads Help

  • Budget. Legacy LSA accounts use an average weekly budget. After migration, Google converts it to a daily average, and monthly spend won’t exceed that daily average times 30.4.
  • Bidding. Pay-per-lead campaigns default to Maximize Conversions, and manual bidding isn’t supported. Google’s guidance is to try Target CPA only once a campaign consistently gets more than 15 leads a month. Source: Google Ads Help

Start the budget where the client can absorb a few weeks of learning. Our LSA budget and bidding guide and Maximize Conversions vs Target CPA go deeper.

Step 8: Set up lead handling before you go live

The ad going live is the easy part. What makes the account work is what happens when the phone rings.

  • Calls. Google charges a missed call during business hours if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds, and a call that hits voicemail during business hours counts as connected. Source: Google Ads Help Know who answers, and what happens when they can’t. See Google now charges for missed calls.
  • Messages and bookings. Google recommends opting in only if the business can respond within 24 hours. Source: Google Ads Help Aim for minutes. See how to respond to LSA message leads.
  • Notifications. After migration, Google opts in every account user and direct manager to lead emails by default, so check who actually needs them. See LSA lead notifications.
  • Feedback. Agree who rates leads and marks them booked. Google uses lead feedback to send more of the leads you want, and responsiveness feeds ranking. See how to rank higher in LSA.

The first 30 days

  1. Week 1. Confirm the ad is actually serving, not stuck in a verification step. Check that test calls ring through to the right number.
  2. Week 2. Read every lead. Turn off service types and areas that produce leads the client doesn’t want.
  3. Weeks 3 and 4. Look at answer rate and booked jobs, not just lead count. Rate leads and mark the booked ones.
  4. Day 30. Send the client a one-page report on leads, cost, answered calls and booked jobs. Our LSA client report template is built for exactly this.

Frequently asked questions

Can I create a new Local Services Ads campaign inside Google Ads?

Not yet. As of November 2026, Google says you can’t create new campaigns with pay-per-lead goals directly in Google Ads. New accounts and additional campaigns still start at g.co/localservices, and Google says campaign creation in Google Ads will come later.

What do you need before setting up Local Services Ads?

An eligible business category in a supported location, a verified Google Business Profile with accurate name, address and hours, a billing method, and whatever documents Google’s screening asks for in that category, which can include a license, insurance, business registration and background checks.

How long does Local Services Ads setup take?

It depends mostly on verification, which varies by category and location. Some businesses qualify for accelerated verification and get 14 days to finish the remaining steps. Pre-badge ads can start after preliminary checks in many categories, but not for garage door services, health care or locksmiths.

Should the agency or the client own the Local Services Ads account?

Either can work, but decide it in writing before launch. The account is tied to the client’s Business Profile and verification, and lead history lives in the account, so spell out who keeps access and data if the relationship ends.

Do I need reviews to run Local Services Ads?

Review requirements depend on the category. Google lists reviews, subject to category requirements, among the steps to go live with pre-badge ads, and recommends aiming for at least 5 reviews on the Business Profile with ratings above four stars.
