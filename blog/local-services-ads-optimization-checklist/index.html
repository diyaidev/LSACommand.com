Google Local Services Ads Optimization: The Checklist We Run on Every Client Account

LSA has no keywords, no ad copy and no negative lists. What you can optimize is a short list, and most of it happens after the lead arrives. Here’s the weekly, monthly and quarterly routine, built on what Google says it ranks.

Most “LSA optimization” advice is a list of profile tips. The tips aren’t wrong, but they skip the part that actually moves an account: whether leads get answered, rated and turned into jobs. For an agency, the hard part isn’t knowing the tips. It’s running them on twenty client accounts every week without one slipping.

The facts below come from Google’s help center, linked as we go. The cadence (weekly, monthly, quarterly) is ours, built from running LSA accounts.

What you can actually optimize

There are no keywords, ad copy or negative keyword lists in Local Services Ads. Google matches the ad to searches from the business’s categories, job types and service area. Its ranking page names three groups of inputs: Source: Google, How ad ranking works

  • Bid: the most you’d pay for a lead.
  • Likelihood of a lead: responsiveness, how relevant the services and bio are to the search, which lead types are enabled, and search context such as time and location. Google notes that missed calls may hurt responsiveness.
  • Profile quality: rating, number of reviews, average response time, and the verification checks completed.

Two details matter for agencies. If a business has several locations, Google shows only the highest-ranking ad for a query, so locations don’t stack on one search. And Google says ranking optimizes for the overall health and diversity of the LSA ecosystem, so nobody can promise a client the top spot.

Weekly: responsiveness and lead feedback

Responsiveness is the input that changes fastest, and the one that costs the most when it slips. Check it every week.

  • Missed calls. Google can charge a missed call during business hours if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. A call that reaches voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours counts as connected. Business hours come from the Google Business Profile, and if the ad schedule is different, unanswered calls are charged only when both are active. Source: Google, How leads work Pull the list of unanswered calls each week and make sure every one got a call back.
  • Message replies. Check the average response time, and check that replies actually reached the customer. Our guide to responding to LSA message leads explains why some never do.
  • Lead feedback. Rate every lead. Google calls its lead feedback survey “crucial” to understanding what you want, and credited leads can include bonus credits for leaving feedback. Source: Google, lead credits
  • Charge status. Charged leads are reassessed over 30 days, and poor-quality, duplicate or invalid ones are credited automatically. Watch the “In review” and “Credited” statuses so the client’s report matches the invoice.

This is the work that produced the result we’re proudest of. Advantage Pools went from critical response weakness to consistent under-30-second responses, and grew lead volume by 300%. Every account is different, but the pattern holds: answering is the cheapest ranking improvement there is.

Monthly: settings, lead types and profile

Job types and service area

Google’s advice is to select every job type the business performs and to set service areas broadly, such as a whole county, rather than individual postal codes. Source: Google, Improve your Local Services Ads performance We’d add one condition: broad works when the client genuinely wants the work everywhere inside it. Google no longer credits “job type not serviced” or “geo not serviced” leads, so a job type or town the client wouldn’t pay for is now a straight cost. Also note that deselecting every job type won’t stop leads on the overall category.

Once a month, sit down with the client: which jobs made money, which towns were too far to drive, which job types they’d happily never see again. Then adjust. Our service area guide shows how to build the footprint city by city.

Business hours and ad schedule

Hours are part of search context, and they now decide when a missed call can be billed. List the hours someone actually answers. If the client can genuinely cover nights and weekends, longer hours open up more searches. If they can’t, an honest schedule protects both the budget and the responsiveness score.

Lead types

Google recommends opting in to message leads, which typically cost less than phone leads, though not always; to direct business search, which lets the ad show when someone searches for the business by name, with no other LSA ad on the page and charges only for new customers; and to booking leads if the client uses an integrated CRM partner. Turn each one on only where the client will respond. A message lead nobody answers drags down response time for the whole account.

Photos and business bio

Google recommends high-quality photos of the team, the work or the equipment. Photos are reviewed before they publish, and you shouldn’t upload photos that identify individuals. The business bio lets you choose up to six highlights. Source: Google, Edit your ad or business information Relevance is a ranking input, and this is the part of the ad the searcher reads.

Reviews

Google recommends 5 or more reviews, and some business types need 5 before the ad will show at all. Beyond that, rating and review count are part of profile quality, and homeowners compare them side by side. Build the review ask into the end of every job rather than running occasional campaigns. Budget and bid mode

Check whether the account hit its monthly max early, and how long it has been in its current bid mode. Google says automated bidding needs about two weeks to settle, so don’t judge a change sooner. Our LSA budget and bidding guide covers the math.

Quarterly and event-driven checks

  • Verification documents. Put insurance and license expiry dates on your calendar, and watch for requests for extra verification steps that land in an inbox nobody reads. Our verification checklist covers what Google checks. One wrinkle: Google’s transition page says insurance and license reverification are no longer required once an account moves into Google Ads, so know which side of the move each client is on.
  • Google Ads migration. The account administrator gets an email 14 days before the move, the old LSA dashboard then redirects to Google Ads, and manual max-per-lead bids end. Check that your reports don’t filter migrated clients out. Source: Google Ads Help, LSA transition More in our Performance Max guide.
  • Access and ownership. Confirm your agency still has the access it needs on every client account. See the manager account guide.
  • Cost per booked job. Once a quarter, work out each client’s cost per booked job. It’s the number that keeps clients, and the one most LSA reports leave out. Our reporting guide covers what to measure.

What not to spend time on

  • Keywords. LSA doesn’t use them, and there’s no negative keyword list. Job types and service area do that job.
  • Daily bid tweaks. Automated bidding needs about two weeks. Constant changes just reset the learning.
  • Old badge claims. Google Guaranteed and Google Screened have been folded into the Google Verified badge. Clean the old wording off client websites. See our Google Verified badge guide.
  • Disputing poor-fit leads one by one. Google no longer credits leads for job types or areas the business doesn’t serve. Fix the settings instead.

If a client’s ads have stopped showing altogether, that’s a different problem with its own order of checks. Start with our not-showing checklist.

Frequently asked questions

How do you optimize Google Local Services Ads?

Answer every call and message quickly, rate every lead, keep job types and service area matched to profitable work, enable message and booking leads only where the team will respond, keep reviews coming, and use a budget and bid mode the client can actually handle.

What are Google’s best practices for Local Services Ads?

Google’s own list: select all job types you perform, set broad service areas, get 5 or more reviews, use Maximize leads bidding, add high-quality photos, and opt in to message leads, direct business search and booking leads where available.

Do keywords matter in Local Services Ads?

No. LSA doesn’t use keywords or negative keywords. Google matches ads to searches from the business’s categories, job types and service area, plus how relevant its services and bio are.

How often should an agency review a client’s LSA account?

We check responsiveness and lead feedback weekly, settings and the profile monthly, and verification, migration status and access quarterly. Responsiveness is the one that can’t wait.

Does a higher budget improve Local Services Ads ranking?

The bid is one ranking input alongside the likelihood of a lead and profile quality. More budget can mean more leads, but it can’t make up for missed calls, slow replies or weak reviews.
