The Local Services Ads Partner Program: What Agencies Can (and Can’t) Join

Search for an LSA “partner” or “reseller” program and you’ll find one, but it isn’t built for agencies. Here’s who Google’s Local Services Ads Partner Program is for, what agencies use instead, and how to sell LSA to clients inside Google’s rules.

Agency owners search for an LSA “partner program” or “reseller program” for a good reason. They want Google’s backing, better support, and a clean way to sell Local Services Ads (LSA) to home-service clients.

Google does run a program with almost exactly that name. It just isn’t built for agencies. This guide explains who it’s for, what agencies can join instead, and how to sell LSA without breaking Google’s rules. It reflects Google’s help pages as of November 2, 2026.

The short answer

  • The Local Services Ads Partner Program is for lead marketplaces. Google describes it as a program that “empowers services marketplaces to scalably generate leads for their professionals.” Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Agencies run LSA from a Google Ads manager account. That’s how you get access to client accounts. See our manager account guide.
  • The badge agencies earn is the Google Partner badge. It comes from the Google Partners program, which covers Google Ads as a whole, not LSA specifically. Source: Google Ads Help

What the LSA Partner Program actually is

Google says marketplaces in the program create, manage and augment “their clients’ Local Service Ads on Google.” According to Google, participating partners can: Source: Google Local Services Help

  • add their own fully attributed content, such as reviews, to their providers’ listings
  • deliver more consumer leads to their providers
  • build a new pay-per-lead revenue stream

The benefits Google lists are:

  • the partner’s name shown on the ads
  • dedicated Google support for optimizing their clients’ campaigns
  • the ability to manage verification for their providers on Google’s behalf

Google’s page ends with an invitation for local services marketplaces to contact Google about eligibility.

You can see the program on the results page. Google says some LSA providers “don’t advertise directly with Google” but through a partner affiliate network. Those listings carry a “from (partner name)” label and the partner’s logo, and their leads go through the partner’s platform rather than Google. Source: Google Local Services Help

Google’s current US list of partner affiliates:

  • Intuit TurboTax
  • Lawyer.com
  • Top Rated Local

The EMEA list is home-services directories such as Checkatrade. These are directories and lead networks, not marketing agencies.

Why it doesn’t fit most agencies

A typical home-service agency doesn’t own a marketplace of providers. Its clients are the advertisers. Each client has its own business, license, Business Profile and Google verification, and each client pays for its own leads.

That matters because Google’s LSA rules are written around the advertiser. Google says advertisers “cannot sell or pass leads to other businesses.” Leads are to be fulfilled only by the advertiser or its vetted technicians, and customer communications should use the same name shown in the ad. Source: Google Advertising Policies Help

So the marketplace model Google describes isn’t something an agency can copy by running its own LSA account and handing the leads out to clients. That’s the lead selling Google prohibits.

The manager account

This is where the work happens. You link each client’s account to your agency’s manager account, set user access levels, and decide in writing who owns the client account. Since Google is moving LSA into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, the manager account is also where you’ll manage migrated clients. Source: Google Ads Help For the step-by-step, see how to manage client LSA from a manager account and managing LSA for multiple clients after Performance Max.

Google Partners: the badge agencies can earn

Google says the Google Partners program “is designed for advertising agencies and third-parties that manage Google Ads accounts on behalf of other brands or businesses.” Signing up from your agency’s manager account enrolls you as a Member. Partner and Premier Partner are tiers you earn later. Source: Google Ads Help

To become a Partner, Google checks three requirements every day: Source: Google Ads Help

RequirementWhat Google asks for
PerformanceAn optimization score of at least 70% on your Google Partners registered manager account
Spend$10,000 USD in ad spend over 90 days across managed accounts
CertificationsAt least 50% of account strategists certified in Google Ads (capped at 100 users), plus one certification in each product area with $500 USD or more in spend over 90 days

Premier Partner is the top 3% of participating companies in a country, judged once a year on client growth, retention, product diversification and annual spend. Source: Google Ads Help

Does LSA spend count toward the $10,000? Google’s requirements page says “ad spend … across managed accounts” and doesn’t break out pay-per-lead campaigns either way. Once a client’s LSA moves into Google Ads it runs as a campaign inside that client’s Google Ads account. Check the Spend figure on your Partners status page before you count on it.

Badge rules worth knowing

Google says only Partners and Premier Partners can display the Google Partner badge. The badge can’t be used in a way that implies Google sponsors or endorses your company, and online use has to link to your Partner profile page. Source: Google Ads Help LSA has its own rule here: you can’t say or imply that you work for Google. Source: Google Advertising Policies Help “Google LSA partner” on an agency’s homepage, without a real program behind it, is a risk.

Is there an LSA certification?

People search for “local service ads certification,” “training” and “course,” so here’s what Google’s own pages show.

Google’s Partners help page describes Google Ads certifications in product areas such as Search, Display, Video, Shopping and Apps, earned through Skillshop. Source: Google Ads Help It doesn’t list a Local Services Ads certification. Neither does the Partners requirements page, which counts certifications by product area with spend. Source: Google Ads Help

Here’s a practical training path for an agency team:

  1. Google Ads Search certification (Skillshop) for the account fundamentals that count toward Partner status.
  2. Google’s LSA help center, especially how leads are charged, the screening rules for each category, and the LSA requirements policy.
  3. The Performance Max transition page, because that’s where LSA settings, budgets and the Leads page now live for migrated clients. Source: Google Ads Help
  4. Hands-on time in a live account: rating leads, handling verification emails and checking charge statuses. No course covers this.

Third-party LSA courses exist too. Treat any course that calls itself an “official LSA certification” with suspicion unless Google lists it.

How to resell LSA the compliant way

“Reselling” LSA as an agency really means selling your management and tooling around the client’s own LSA account. The rules that keep it clean:

  1. One advertiser, one account. Google’s third-party policy requires “a separate account for each end-advertiser that you manage.” Source: Google Advertising Policies Help
  2. The client pays for its own leads. Charges post to the client’s account, even when your agency’s card or invoice covers them. See how LSA billing works for agencies.
  3. Be transparent about costs. Google’s third-party policy says you need to be “transparent and honest” about your company, your services, the costs of those services and the results clients can expect. Source: Google Advertising Policies Help Show ad spend separately from your fee. For fee structures, see how to price LSA management.
  4. Never pass leads around. No routing one client’s LSA leads to another business, and no call centers forwarding to businesses that aren’t in the ad. Source: Google Advertising Policies Help
  5. Don’t claim Google status you don’t have. No Partner badge before you’ve earned it, and no “official Google LSA partner” wording.

Inside those rules there’s plenty of room to build a real offer. That means onboarding and verification, answering leads, rating leads to win credits, review requests and monthly reporting, all under your brand. Our white-label LSA guide covers how agencies structure it.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a Google Local Services Ads partner program for agencies?

Google’s Local Services Ads Partner Program is aimed at local services marketplaces that generate and sell leads for the professionals on their platform. Agencies usually work through a Google Ads manager account and, separately, the Google Partners program.

How do I become a Google Partner?

Join Google Partners from your agency’s Google Ads manager account, which makes you a Member. To earn the Partner badge, Google requires a 70% optimization score on the registered manager account, $10,000 USD in ad spend over 90 days across managed accounts, and at least 50% of account strategists certified.

Is there a Google Local Services Ads certification?

Google’s Partners help pages list certification product areas such as Search, Display, Video, Shopping and Apps. They don’t list a Local Services Ads certification. Google’s LSA training lives in the Local Services and Google Ads help centers.

Can an agency resell Local Services Ads?

An agency can manage LSA for clients and charge for that work. It can’t buy LSA leads and sell them on: Google’s LSA policy says advertisers can’t sell or pass leads to other businesses, and each advertiser needs its own account.

Can I say my agency is a Google LSA partner?

Only say what’s true. Google says only Partners and Premier Partners can display the Google Partner badge, and its LSA policy prohibits implying you work for Google. Being in a manager account or using an LSA tool doesn’t make you a Google partner.
