How to Price Local Services Ads Management for Your Clients

Local Services Ads are awkward to price. Google bills per lead, good work can lower the ad spend, and the part that decides results, answering the leads, isn’t in Google Ads at all. Here are the pricing models agencies use, which one fits LSA, what to include at each tier, and the margin math.

Most agencies price Local Services Ads the way they price Google search ads: a percentage of spend, or a flat retainer copied from their PPC offer. Both can work, but LSA is a different product, and pricing it like PPC can leave you underpaid for the work that actually matters.

This guide is for agency owners deciding what to charge. It covers your cost to serve, the five common pricing models and their LSA-specific traps, what to include at each tier, a worked margin example, and the Google rules that apply to any management fee.

Start with your cost to serve

Before you pick a model, work out what one client costs you each month. For LSA, that’s usually:

  • Management time: weekly checks on budget, bids, service area and lead flow; monthly reporting; client calls.
  • Lead response: whoever answers calls and replies to message leads when the client doesn’t. This is often the biggest cost, and the one most quotes leave out.
  • Software: call answering, alerts, reporting and any tools you use across accounts.
  • Onboarding: sign-up, verification and the Business Profile link. Front-loaded and often slow, because some steps only the owner can complete.
  • Change overhead: this year that means the move into Google Ads, missed-call billing and the new credit rules.

Then use a simple floor:

Minimum monthly fee = (hours per month × your loaded hourly cost + tools per client) ÷ (1 − your target margin)

If a price doesn’t clear that floor at your real hours, no model will save it.

Why percent of spend is awkward for LSA

On search ads, more spend usually means more work. On LSA, a tighter service area, cleaner job types and better lead ratings can lower what the client spends for the same jobs. If your fee is a share of spend, that improvement cuts your pay. After migration, Google also shows the budget as a daily average, the weekly budget divided by 7, so spend-based invoices need explaining. Source: Google Ads Help

Why per-lead pricing needs care

Google marks each lead with a charge status, and can keep reviewing a lead’s quality for 30 days after it arrives. Source: Google Local Services Help If you bill per lead, bill on leads Google actually charged, after that window, or reconcile credits on the next invoice. And a per-lead markup is still a management fee, so the disclosure rules below apply.

Which model fits LSA best

For most agencies, the cleanest structure for LSA is:

  1. Ad spend paid by the client to Google, on the client’s own payment method or through your consolidated billing, shown as its own line.
  2. A flat monthly fee per LSA account, tiered by how much of the job you take on.
  3. An optional one-time setup fee for new accounts that still need sign-up and verification.

A flat per-account fee matches how the work behaves: it scales with the number of accounts and how much lead response you cover, not with the size of the client’s budget. It also keeps your incentive simple. You get paid to make every lead count, not to make the budget bigger.

What to include at each tier

Define tiers by scope, not by budget. A three-tier menu works for most books:

How to present LSA pricing to a client

  • Separate ad spend from your fee. The client should always see what Google charged and what you charge as two different lines.
  • Explain pay-per-lead and credits in one sentence. “You pay Google per lead, not per click, and we rate every lead so the bad ones can be credited.”
  • Set expectations for the move into Google Ads. Google says to allow up to two weeks after migration for performance to ramp back to stable levels. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Sell the response guarantee, not the dashboard. “Every lead gets answered” is easier to buy than “we optimize your bids.”
  • Report outcomes. Leads answered, booked and credited. Our guide to LSA reporting for agencies covers what a useful client report includes.

Google’s rules on management fees

Google’s third-party policies apply to any agency buying ads for clients. Build your pricing so it passes them: Source: Google Advertising Policies Help

  • Disclose the fee in writing before the first purchase, and show it on every customer invoice.
  • Report Google’s cost exactly, at the amount Google charged, not including your fees.
  • Provide the client’s Google Ads customer ID when they ask for it.
  • Share Google’s disclosure notice if most of your clients are small. If 80% or more of your customers spend less than $1,000 a month on Google Ads, Google requires you to share its “Working with third parties” advertiser guide with all of them.

Google also expects third parties to be honest about their costs and expected results. Source: Google Advertising Policies Help Don’t promise a lead count you can’t control.

Frequently asked questions

How should an agency charge for Local Services Ads management?

For most agencies, a flat monthly fee per LSA account works best, tiered by scope, with ad spend paid separately to Google and an optional setup fee for new accounts. It scales with the work rather than with the client’s budget.

Is percent of ad spend a good model for LSA?

It can be for large, growing budgets, but it’s awkward for LSA. Better lead quality and credits can lower spend, so your fee drops when you do good work.

Can I mark up LSA leads?

You can charge per lead, but it’s still a management fee under Google’s third-party policy. Disclose it in writing before the first purchase, show it on every invoice, and report Google’s cost exactly, without your fees.

What should an LSA management package include?

At minimum: setup and verification help, budget and bidding, service area and job types, weekly checks and a monthly report. Higher tiers add lead rating, reviews, recording archives and, most valuable, answering every call and message lead.
