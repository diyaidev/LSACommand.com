How to Pause Google Local Services Ads (Before and After Performance Max)

A client is booked solid, on vacation, or behind on payment, and you need the leads to stop today. Where the pause switch lives now depends on whether the account has moved into Google Ads. Here’s how to pause in both places, the softer options, and the things that pause ads on their own.

Sooner or later every agency gets the call: “Turn it off, we can’t take any more jobs.” Or the client is on vacation, or the account is unpaid. Pausing Local Services Ads (LSA) takes a minute, but the switch moved in 2026, and there are cases where a full pause isn’t the best answer.

This guide covers both places the switch can be, the gentler options, and the things that pause a client’s ads without anyone touching them. It reflects Google’s help pages as of November 1, 2026.

First: has the account moved to Google Ads?

Google is moving LSA accounts into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, in phases through late 2026 and 2027. The account administrator gets an email 14 days before the move, and after it Google says signing in to the old LSA dashboard “will automatically redirect you to your campaign overview in Google Ads.” Source: Google Ads Help

So the quick test is to sign in to the client’s LSA dashboard. If you land in Google Ads, use the Google Ads steps. If you land in the old lead inbox, use the dashboard steps.

How to pause in Google Ads (migrated accounts)

Google’s steps for a migrated account: Source: Google Ads Help

  1. Open the client’s Google Ads account.
  2. Go to the Settings tab on the Campaigns page.
  3. Select the pay-per-lead campaign.
  4. Set the campaign status to Paused.

To resume, set the status back to Enabled. If the client has several pay-per-lead campaigns, for example one per category, pause each one you want stopped.

How to pause in the LSA dashboard (not yet migrated)

For accounts still on the original dashboard: Source: Google Local Services Help

  1. Sign in to the Local Services Ads lead inbox.
  2. Click the menu icon in the top left.
  3. Select Profile & budget.
  4. Switch the ad’s status off with the toggle.

Switching it back on resumes the ad on whatever ad schedule you’ve set. Source: Google Local Services Help Google’s billing page also says to pause or edit these campaigns from the LSA inbox “rather than the main Google Ads interface,” so don’t go looking for the switch in Google Ads before the account has migrated. Source: Google Ads Help

What happens while the ad is paused

  • The ad stops showing, but Google says “a free version of your listing may be displayed” while the ad is paused or outside its schedule. Source: Google Local Services Help
  • Paused ads don’t accrue charges, according to Google Ads. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Lead history stays. Past leads remain in the account. For migrated accounts, Google says lead history, messages and older call recordings move to the new Leads page. Source: Google Ads Help
  • Ranking. Google doesn’t document a ranking penalty for pausing. It does say responsiveness affects ranking and missed calls can hurt it. Source: Google Local Services Help If nobody can answer, pausing is usually the safer move.

Five ways to slow leads without a full pause

A full pause is a blunt tool. Often the client only wants fewer leads, or none at certain times.

1. Change the ad schedule

Google lets you run the ad all day, only during business hours, or on custom hours. Source: Google Local Services Help In migrated accounts the ad schedule is in campaign settings and can be set separately from the Business Profile. If the problem is nights and weekends, schedule around them instead of pausing all week.

2. Lower the budget

Fewer dollars means fewer leads. Google says that once the monthly max is reached, the ad won’t appear for the rest of the month unless the budget changes. Source: Google Ads Help It’s a reasonable dial for a busy season, but not an off switch.

3. Turn off service types

If the client is overloaded with one kind of job, switch off those service types and keep the rest. Just know the limit: Google says “deselecting all job types won’t stop you from getting leads on the overall category. You must pause your ad to stop receiving all leads.” Source: Google Local Services Help

4. Tighten the service area

Drop the far edges of the area to cut volume while keeping the core towns. Google warns that slow response from distant areas can hurt ranking anyway. See our service area targeting guide.

5. Turn off message leads

If the team can’t keep up with texts, message leads can be switched off on their own. Google recommends opting in only when the business can respond within 24 hours. Source: Google Ads Help

On the old dashboard there’s also a “temporarily closed” status under business hours, which Google says you can select “if you want to turn off your leads.” Source: Google Local Services Help

Pause, don’t remove

Google Ads has a separate “remove” option for campaigns. If there’s any chance the client comes back, pause instead. Google also says new pay-per-lead campaigns can’t be created directly in Google Ads yet; new ones still start at g.co/localservices. Source: Google Ads Help So a migrated campaign you remove isn’t one you can simply rebuild in Google Ads.

For agencies: a pause policy

Pauses are where agencies lose track of clients. A few rules help:

  1. Get the request in writing. A text or email with the date is enough. It protects you when the client later asks why leads dropped.
  2. Log every pause in your client tracker: who asked, why, the date, and a resume date.
  3. Set a resume reminder. A paused account with no resume date tends to stay paused, and the client quietly churns.
  4. Offer the partial option first. “Want us to pause nights and weekends instead?” keeps more of the account running.
  5. Agree the non-payment rule upfront. If you pause for unpaid invoices, say so in the agreement, and pause rather than remove.

Frequently asked questions

How do I pause Google Local Services Ads after the move to Google Ads?

Go to the Settings tab on the Campaigns page in Google Ads, select the pay-per-lead campaign and set its status to Paused. Set it back to Enabled to resume.

How do I pause Local Services Ads in the old dashboard?

In the Local Services Ads lead inbox, open the menu, choose Profile & budget, and switch the ad’s status off with the toggle. Switch it back on to resume on your ad schedule.

Am I charged while Local Services Ads are paused?

Google Ads says ads don’t accrue charges while they’re paused. Google also notes that a free version of the listing may still show while the ad is paused.

Will turning off all job types stop Local Services Ads leads?

No. Google says deselecting all job types won’t stop leads on the overall category. You have to pause the ad to stop all leads.

Does pausing Local Services Ads hurt ranking?

Google doesn’t document a ranking penalty for pausing. It does say missed calls can hurt responsiveness, which affects ranking, so pausing when nobody can answer is usually safer than leaving the ad on.
