How to read this

  • Margin here is narrow on purpose. It’s your management fee minus the LSA work this page counts: lead review and rating, disputes and credits, and reporting, plus LSA Command where it applies. Ad spend, onboarding, client calls and your other overhead aren’t in it.
  • Break-even is the honest test. LSA Command costs $200 per client, so on labor alone it pays for itself only when it saves at least $200 ÷ your hourly cost in hours per client each month. Below that, your manual process is cheaper, and the calculator says so.
  • Lead volume drives almost everything. Review time scales with leads, so low-volume clients rarely justify automation on labor alone, and high-volume clients usually do.
  • Freed hours are only worth money if you use them, for more clients on the same team or fewer contractor hours.
  • The minimum fee line is the floor from our pricing method: (hours × loaded hourly cost + tools per client) ÷ (1 − target margin). Pricing below it loses money at your real hours.

What the labor math leaves out

Some of what clients pay an agency for doesn’t fit in an hour count: calls answered when the client’s team doesn’t pick up, instant lead alerts, notes on every lead, review requests, and blocked robocalls. Missed calls also cost the client money directly; the missed-call cost calculator puts a number on that. If your result is close to break-even, those are what decide it, and they’re worth weighing against client churn.

Assumptions

  • Every client has the same lead count and the same time per task. Use averages across your book.
  • The “with LSA Command” times are placeholders for you to change. We haven’t measured them across agencies, so we don’t present them as results.
  • Full-time equivalent uses 160 working hours a month.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours does LSA management take per client?

It depends mostly on lead volume. Reviewing and rating leads by hand scales with every call and message, while disputes and reporting are closer to a fixed monthly cost. Enter your own times above to see the total for your book.

What should an agency charge for LSA management?

Start from your cost to serve. A simple floor is (hours per month × loaded hourly cost + tools per client) ÷ (1 − target margin). The calculator shows that floor for both the manual and the automated setup.
