How to read this

  • The big number is cash already spent. It’s what the client paid Google for calls that reached nobody. That’s the part of the bill an agency can fix this month, by changing hours and call routing rather than bids.
  • Revenue at risk is an upper bound. It assumes every missed caller would have booked at the client’s normal close rate. Some would have hired a competitor anyway, and some call back. Use “Missed callers you still book” if the client returns calls.
  • Don’t add the two together. Wasted spend is money out. Revenue at risk is gross revenue, not profit.
  • The “10 more of every 100 calls” line shows what a realistic improvement is worth, which is usually an easier conversation with a client than the worst case.

The billing rule behind the math

Under Google’s lead rules, a missed call is a charged lead when it comes in during the hours on the client’s Business Profile (and inside the ad schedule, if one is set) and the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. A call that reaches voicemail or an automated greeting counts as connected, so it can be charged after 20 seconds whether or not the caller leaves a message. With a phone menu, the 20 seconds start at the first key press. The same phone number isn’t charged again within 15 days. Source: Google Local Services Help, how leads work.

Assumptions

  • Every charged missed call costs the average cost per lead you enter. Individual lead prices vary, so the result is an average, not an invoice line.
  • Repeat callers: because a number isn’t charged twice inside 15 days, a client with many repeat missed callers has fewer charges than missed calls. Lower “Missed calls Google charges” to match.
  • Missed calls outside Business Profile hours or the ad schedule aren’t charged under this rule. They still cost jobs, so they stay in the revenue line.
  • Nothing you type leaves your browser. “Copy link” puts the numbers in the part of the link after the #, which isn’t sent to any server.

What to fix first

  1. Make the Business Profile hours match the hours someone is actually on the phones.
  2. Set the ad schedule to the hours the client can cover.
  3. Send unanswered calls to something that answers before the 20-second mark, and stop using voicemail as the overflow. LSA Command’s AI call answering picks up when the client’s team doesn’t.
  4. Rate every lead and track credits for spam and invalid ones. See lead disputes and credits.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google charge for missed calls on Local Services Ads?

Yes, in some cases. A missed call is charged when it arrives during the hours on the Business Profile (and inside the ad schedule, if one is set) and the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds.

Does a call that goes to voicemail count as missed?

Google counts a call that reaches voicemail or an automated greeting as connected, so it can be charged after 20 seconds whether or not the caller leaves a message. Voicemail is not a safe overflow for LSA calls.

How do I find missed-call charges on a client’s bill?

Pull every charged phone lead for the last full month. Flag recordings with no conversation (ringing or the voicemail greeting) and charges at times nobody was on the phones, then match them against the client’s own call log.
