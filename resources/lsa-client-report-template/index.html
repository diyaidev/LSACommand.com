The two traps that make an LSA report wrong

Trap 1: “Conversions” is not “leads”. Google’s migration page says the Conversions column on the Campaigns page displays the total number of leads you’re charged for. The Leads page shows every lead, with a Charge Status of Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited. If the client gets a lead alert for 31 leads and your report says 24, it looks like you’re hiding leads, when the gap is actually leads they didn’t pay for. Report both.

Trap 2: last month isn’t closed yet. Google says it continuously assesses lead quality over a 30-day period and credits poor, duplicate or invalid leads automatically (except for accounts in EEA countries). A report sent on the 1st can change after the client reads it. Mark it provisional, show how many leads are still in review, and restate last month at the top of the next report.

What changed under Performance Max

Six things clients get confused about in the first months after migration. Answer them in the report before they ask.

“Conversions” means charged leads

Google’s migration page says the Conversions column displays the total number of leads you’re charged for. Report leads received from the Leads page next to it, or the client sees fewer leads than they got alerts for.

Last month isn’t final for 30 days

Google continuously assesses lead quality over a 30-day period and credits poor, duplicate or invalid leads automatically (not in EEA countries). Mark the report provisional and restate last month at the top of the next one.

Count leads, not calls

Once a lead is charged, follow-up calls or messages from the same number or email within 15 days aren’t charged again.

No history inside Google Ads

Google says previous LSA performance reports won’t carry over. Compare against your own archive, or start the baseline now.

Expect a ramp after migration

Google says to allow up to two weeks for migration to complete and performance to ramp back to stable levels. Put the migration date on the report.

Leave clicks out of the headline

You pay for valid leads, not ad clicks. Clicks and click-through rate don’t cost the client anything, so don’t judge the account on them.

Where each number lives (about 10 minutes per client)

  • Campaigns page: set the date range to the month. Add Conversions, Cost and Cost / conv. Write down those three (section 03, and charged leads in 02).
  • Goals › Conversions › Leads: same date range. Download the leads (Google says you can download them to import into your own CRM). In a sheet, pivot on Charge Status, Lead Type and Service Type. That fills sections 01, 02 and 04.
  • Lead status: count leads marked Booked or Completed for section 05. Google says updating these statuses helps its systems optimize the campaign, so get the client’s office doing it weekly.
  • Calls answered (06): pull it from wherever calls actually land: the client’s phone system, answering service or the call recordings. [VERIFY in a migrated account: whether the Leads page labels missed or unanswered calls. If it does, use it as a cross-check.]
  • Recordings: save 2 or 3 calls worth playing for the client (a great call, a lead that went to voicemail). Google keeps call recordings for up to 30 days.
  • Reviews (07): note the Business Profile rating and review count on the first and last day of the month.
  • Budget (08): daily average budget × 30.4 = monthly cap. Compare it with Cost for the month. Note any day the campaign hit the monthly maximum.
  • Send on the same day every month. If you send on the 1st, mark it provisional and show the in-review count.

Running it across your client roster

Running 20 clients? Keep one master sheet, one row per client, with the same numbers. Then read down the Answer rate column: clients whose calls aren’t reaching a person are the most likely to blame the ads and cancel, and the easiest conversation about fixing lead response.

The spreadsheet has a Roster tab with these columns, and there’s a roster CSV if you’d rather start from a blank sheet. For the tooling side, see LSA reporting software for agencies.
